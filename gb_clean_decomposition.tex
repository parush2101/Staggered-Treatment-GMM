\title{%
  Redundancy of Forbidden Comparisons\\
  and a Clean Goodman--Bacon Decomposition%
}
\author{Parush Arora \and Rishabh Bijani\\
  \small Department of Economics, Ashoka University}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
The Goodman--Bacon (2021) decomposition shows that the two-way fixed effects
(TWFE) estimator is a weighted average of \emph{all} pairwise $2\times 2$
difference-in-differences (DiD) comparisons, including \emph{forbidden} ones
that use already-treated cohorts as controls. We prove a sample-level
algebraic identity: every forbidden comparison---which uses an already-treated
control cohort---equals the difference of two never-treated comparisons
evaluated at the same calendar periods, with the never-treated terms cancelling
exactly. This identity implies that forbidden comparisons are
\emph{redundant}: they contain no information beyond what is already encoded
in the clean comparisons. Exploiting the identity, we derive a \emph{clean
Goodman--Bacon decomposition} that represents TWFE as a signed weighted sum of
clean DiDs alone, recovering TWFE exactly (to machine precision) in every
sample. We characterise the new effective weights and show that already-treated
cohorts enter with \emph{negative} effective weight. Under homogeneous,
time-constant treatment effects, the negative-weight terms vanish in
expectation and TWFE is unbiased. Under heterogeneous effects, the same
negative-weight terms are non-zero and constitute the exact source of TWFE's
bias. The clean decomposition therefore does not remove bias; it exposes its
structural origin and shows what an unbiased alternative must discard.
Exploiting the redundancy result, we introduce a GMM estimator that uses only
clean comparisons and achieves minimum asymptotic variance under arbitrary
within-unit serial correlation by optimally weighting the moment covariance
matrix.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup and Notation}
\label{sec:setup}
%%%============================================================================

We observe the balanced panel $\{Y_{igt},D_{it}\}_{i=1}^{N}$ over $T$
calendar periods indexed $t\in\{1,\ldots,T\}$. Here $Y_{igt}\in\mathbb{R}$ is
the outcome of unit $i$ in cohort $g$ at time $t$, and $D_{it}\in\{0,1\}$ is a
binary treatment indicator. Units are partitioned into cohorts indexed by their
first treatment date. We let $g=\infty$ index the \emph{never-treated} cohort
and $g\in\{2,\ldots,T\}$ index the $G$ treated cohorts. Let $N_g$ denote the
number of units in cohort $g$, with $\sum_{g}N_g=N$. The within-cohort average
outcome at time $t$ is
\begin{equation}
  Y_{g,t} \;=\; \frac{1}{N_g}\sum_{i\in g}Y_{igt}.
  \label{eq:cohort_avg}
\end{equation}
For any set of calendar periods $\mathcal{S}$, let $\bY_{g,\mathcal{S}}=
|\mathcal{S}|^{-1}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{S}}Y_{g,t}$ denote the cohort--period
average.

\medskip
We maintain four standard assumptions.

\begin{assumption}[Cross-Sectional Independence]
  $\{Y_{ig1},\ldots,Y_{igT},D_{i1},\ldots,D_{iT}\}_{i=1}^{N}$ are
  independent and identically distributed across $i$; serial dependence
  within units is unrestricted.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Irreversibility of Treatment]
  $D_{i,t-1}=1$ implies $D_{it}=1$ for all $i,t$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Parallel Trends]
  For all cohorts $g,g'$ and all $t>t'$:
  \[
    \E[Y_{igt}(\infty)]-\E[Y_{igt'}(\infty)]
    \;=\;
    \E[Y_{ig't}(\infty)]-\E[Y_{ig't'}(\infty)],
  \]
  where $Y_{igt}(\infty)$ denotes the potential outcome under no treatment.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[No Anticipation]
  $\E[Y_{igt}\mid D_{it}=0]=\E[Y_{igt}(\infty)]$ for all $t<g$.
\end{assumption}

\subsection*{Cohort-and-time ATT and aggregate ATT}

The cohort-and-time-specific average treatment effect on the treated (CATT)
for cohort $g$ at event horizon $k\geq0$ is
\begin{equation}
  \beta_{g,g+k}
  \;=\;
  \E\!\bigl[Y_{ig,g+k}(1)-Y_{ig,g+k}(\infty)\mid G_i=g\bigr],
  \label{eq:catt}
\end{equation}
where $Y_{igt}(1)$ is the potential outcome under treatment and $G_i=g$ denotes
cohort membership. The aggregate ATT is a researcher-chosen weighted average of
CATTs,
\begin{equation}
  \theta \;=\; \sum_{g}\sum_{k\geq0}w_{g,g+k}\,\beta_{g,g+k},
  \qquad \sum_{g}\sum_{k\geq0}w_{g,g+k}=1,
  \label{eq:att}
\end{equation}
with the standard choice weighting each $(g,t)$ cell by its share of treated
observations. The TWFE regression
\begin{equation}
  Y_{igt} = \alpha_i + \lambda_t + \theta\,D_{it} + \varepsilon_{it}
  \label{eq:twfe}
\end{equation}
produces a consistent estimator of $\theta$ when CATTs are homogeneous across
all $(g,t)$ cells. Under heterogeneous CATTs, $\TWFE$ is generally biased, as
we analyse in detail in Section~\ref{sec:hetero}.

%%%============================================================================
\section{Two-by-Two DiD Comparisons}
\label{sec:2x2}
%%%============================================================================

We index $2\times2$ DiD comparisons by the focal cohort $g$, control cohort
$c$, post-period calendar time $g+k$ ($k\geq0$), and pre-period lag $m\geq1$.
The sample $2\times2$ DiD is
\begin{equation}
  \hbeta^{cm}_{g,g+k}
  \;=\;
  (Y_{g,g+k}-Y_{g,g-m})-(Y_{c,g+k}-Y_{c,g-m}).
  \label{eq:2x2}
\end{equation}
Three types of control cohort arise in the staggered design.

\begin{definition}[Clean and Forbidden Comparisons]
  \label{def:types}
  The $2\times2$ DiD $\hbeta^{cm}_{g,g+k}$ in \eqref{eq:2x2} is:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Never-treated} (clean, $c=\infty$): the control group is
      never treated,
      \[
        \hbeta^{\infty m}_{g,g+k}
        = (Y_{g,g+k}-Y_{g,g-m})-(Y_{\infty,g+k}-Y_{\infty,g-m}).
      \]
    \item \textbf{Not-yet-treated} (clean, $c=g+l$, $l\in\{k+1,\ldots,T-g\}$):
      the control cohort has not yet received treatment by the post period
      $g+k$,
      \[
        \hbeta^{lm}_{g,g+k}
        = (Y_{g,g+k}-Y_{g,g-m})-(Y_{g+l,g+k}-Y_{g+l,g-m}).
      \]
    \item \textbf{Already-treated} (\emph{forbidden}, $c=g-j$,
      $j\in\{m+1,\ldots,g-1\}$): the control cohort's treatment date $g-j$
      strictly precedes the pre-period reference date $g-m$, so the control
      group is treated throughout both the pre- and post-windows of the
      comparison,
      \[
        \hbeta^{jm}_{g,g+k}
        = (Y_{g,g+k}-Y_{g,g-m})-(Y_{g-j,g+k}-Y_{g-j,g-m}).
      \]
  \end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Identification under homogeneous effects.}
When CATTs are homogeneous ($\beta_{g,g+k}=\theta$ for all $g,k$), all three
types of $2\times2$ DiD satisfy $\E[\hbeta^{cm}_{g,g+k}]=\theta$ under
Assumptions~1--4. The sections that follow (Sections~\ref{sec:gb}--\ref{sec:clean_gb})
establish algebraic results---the Goodman-Bacon decomposition and the clean
re-expression of TWFE---that hold regardless of whether effects are
homogeneous or heterogeneous. Section~\ref{sec:hetero} then analyses what
these results imply when CATTs are heterogeneous.

\paragraph{Bias of the forbidden comparison under heterogeneous effects.}
When CATTs are heterogeneous, the expected value of the forbidden comparison is
\begin{equation}
  \E\bigl[\hbeta^{jm}_{g,g+k}\bigr]
  \;=\;
  \beta_{g,g+k}
  - \bigl(\beta_{g-j,\,g+k} - \beta_{g-j,\,g-m}\bigr).
  \label{eq:bias}
\end{equation}
The bias equals the change in the already-treated control cohort's own CATT
between the pre- and post-periods of the comparison. Clean comparisons do not
suffer this contamination: $\beta_{\infty,t}=0$ for all $t$, and
$\beta_{g+l,\,g+k}=0$ because the not-yet-treated control is untreated at
$g+k$.

%%%============================================================================
\section{The Goodman--Bacon Decomposition}
\label{sec:gb}
%%%============================================================================

Goodman-Bacon (2021) establishes that $\TWFE$ is a weighted average of all
pairwise $2\times2$ DiDs that can be formed from the panel. The decomposition
groups these into three aggregate objects, each an equal-weighted average of
the period-specific DiDs from Definition~\ref{def:types}.

\subsection*{Aggregate DiD objects}

For each treated cohort $g$, the \textbf{treated-vs-never-treated DiD} is the
equal-weighted average of the period-specific never-treated DiDs
(Definition~\ref{def:types}(1)) over all post-periods $g+k\in\{g,\ldots,T\}$
and pre-lags $m\in\{1,\ldots,g-1\}$:
\begin{equation}
  \hDelta_{g,\infty}
  \;=\;
  \frac{1}{(T-g+1)(g-1)}
  \sum_{k=0}^{T-g}\sum_{m=1}^{g-1}
  \hbeta^{\infty m}_{g,\,g+k}.
  \label{eq:did_nt}
\end{equation}

For each early--late pair $(g_e,g_l)$ with $g_e<g_l$, two aggregate $2\times2$
DiDs arise. The \textbf{``Earlier vs.\ Later Treated''} comparison averages the
not-yet-treated DiDs (Definition~\ref{def:types}(2)) with focal cohort $g_e$
and control $g_l$ over the $(g_l-g_e)(g_e-1)$ period-by-lag pairs where $g_l$
is not yet treated:
\begin{equation}
  \hDelta_{g_e,g_l}^{E}
  \;=\;
  \frac{1}{(g_l-g_e)(g_e-1)}
  \sum_{k=0}^{g_l-g_e-1}\sum_{m=1}^{g_e-1}
  \bigl[(Y_{g_e,g_e+k}-Y_{g_e,g_e-m})-(Y_{g_l,g_e+k}-Y_{g_l,g_e-m})\bigr].
  \label{eq:did_early}
\end{equation}
At every post-period $g_e+k$ in this sum, the control cohort $g_l$ satisfies
$g_l > g_e+k$ and is therefore not yet treated.

The \textbf{``Later vs.\ Earlier Treated''} comparison averages the
already-treated DiDs (Definition~\ref{def:types}(3)) with focal cohort $g_l$
and control $g_e$ over the $(T-g_l+1)(g_l-g_e)$ period-by-lag pairs:
\begin{equation}
  \hDelta_{g_l,g_e}^{F}
  \;=\;
  \frac{1}{(T-g_l+1)(g_l-g_e)}
  \sum_{k=0}^{T-g_l}\sum_{m=1}^{g_l-g_e}
  \bigl[(Y_{g_l,g_l+k}-Y_{g_l,g_l-m})-(Y_{g_e,g_l+k}-Y_{g_e,g_l-m})\bigr].
  \label{eq:did_forbidden}
\end{equation}
In $\hDelta_{g_l,g_e}^F$, cohort $g_e$ serves as control despite being already
treated at every pre-period $g_l-m\geq g_e$ in this average.

\subsection*{The decomposition}

\begin{theorem}[Goodman-Bacon Decomposition; Goodman-Bacon, 2021]
  \label{thm:gb}
  Under Assumptions~1--2, the TWFE estimator satisfies
  \begin{equation}
    \TWFE
    \;=\;
    \sum_{g\neq\infty}w_{g\infty}\,\hDelta_{g,\infty}
    \;+\;
    \sum_{g_e<g_l}\!
      \Bigl[
        w_{g_e g_l}^{E}\,\hDelta_{g_e,g_l}^{E}
        +
        w_{g_l g_e}^{F}\,\hDelta_{g_l,g_e}^{F}
      \Bigr],
    \label{eq:gb}
  \end{equation}
  where all weights are non-negative and sum to one:
  \[
    \sum_{g\neq\infty}w_{g\infty}
    +\sum_{g_e<g_l}\!\bigl(w_{g_e g_l}^{E}+w_{g_l g_e}^{F}\bigr)
    \;=\;1.
  \]
  The weights depend on cohort sizes and treatment timing. For the
  treated-vs-never-treated term,
  \[
    w_{g\infty}
    \;\propto\;
    N_g N_\infty \cdot
    \frac{(T-g+1)(g-1)}{T^2};
  \]
  see Goodman-Bacon (2021, Theorem~1) for the full weight formulas.
\end{theorem}

\noindent
All weights are non-negative and TWFE genuinely relies on the forbidden DiDs
$\hDelta_{g_l,g_e}^F$, which receive strictly positive weight $w_{g_l g_e}^F>0$.
Under homogeneous effects this is harmless. Under heterogeneous CATTs it
induces bias via \eqref{eq:bias}, as we show in Section~\ref{sec:hetero}.

%%%============================================================================
\section{Redundancy of Forbidden Comparisons}
\label{sec:redundancy}
%%%============================================================================

We now prove the key algebraic result. Every forbidden comparison---which uses
an already-treated cohort as control---equals the difference of two
never-treated comparisons evaluated at the same calendar periods. The identity
is a sample-level fact, independent of any assumption about the homogeneity or
heterogeneity of treatment effects.

\begin{lemma}[Algebraic Identity for Forbidden Comparisons]
  \label{lem:identity}
  Consider any forbidden comparison $\hbeta^{jm}_{g_l,g_l+k}$ with
  already-treated control cohort $g_e = g_l - j$, post calendar period
  $g_l + k$, and pre calendar period $g_l - m$. Define the
  \emph{co-period never-treated DiD} for the already-treated cohort $g_e$:
  \begin{equation}
    \hpsi^{\infty m}_{g_e,\,g_l+k}
    \;\equiv\;
    (Y_{g_e,\,g_l+k} - Y_{g_e,\,g_l-m})
    - (Y_{\infty,\,g_l+k} - Y_{\infty,\,g_l-m}),
    \label{eq:copd}
  \end{equation}
  which is a never-treated DiD for focal cohort $g_e$ using the \emph{same
  calendar periods} $(g_l+k,\,g_l-m)$ as the forbidden comparison.
  Then the following algebraic identity holds in every finite sample:
  \begin{equation}
    \boxed{%
      \hbeta^{jm}_{g_l,\,g_l+k}
      \;=\;
      \hbeta^{\infty m}_{g_l,\,g_l+k}
      \;-\;
      \hpsi^{\infty m}_{g_e,\,g_l+k}.
    }
    \label{eq:identity}
  \end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Expand both never-treated DiDs on the right-hand side and subtract:
  \begin{align*}
    \hbeta^{\infty m}_{g_l,g_l+k} - \hpsi^{\infty m}_{g_e,g_l+k}
    &\;=\;
    \bigl[(Y_{g_l,g_l+k}-Y_{g_l,g_l-m})-(Y_{\infty,g_l+k}-Y_{\infty,g_l-m})\bigr]
    \\
    &\quad
    -\bigl[(Y_{g_e,g_l+k}-Y_{g_e,g_l-m})-(Y_{\infty,g_l+k}-Y_{\infty,g_l-m})\bigr]
    \\
    &\;=\;
    (Y_{g_l,g_l+k}-Y_{g_l,g_l-m})-(Y_{g_e,g_l+k}-Y_{g_e,g_l-m})
    \\
    &\quad
    +\underbrace{(Y_{\infty,g_l+k}-Y_{\infty,g_l-m})-(Y_{\infty,g_l+k}-Y_{\infty,g_l-m})}_{=\;0}
    \\
    &\;=\;
    (Y_{g_l,g_l+k}-Y_{g_l,g_l-m})-(Y_{g_e,g_l+k}-Y_{g_e,g_l-m})
    \;=\;
    \hbeta^{jm}_{g_l,g_l+k},
  \end{align*}
  where the last equality uses Definition~\ref{def:types}(3) with $g_e=g_l-j$.
  The never-treated outcomes cancel \emph{exactly} in every sample because they
  appear with opposite signs. No approximation or probability limit is used.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  The identity \eqref{eq:identity} has a transparent control-group
  interpretation. The forbidden comparison $\hbeta^{jm}_{g_l,g_l+k}$ measures
  the change in $g_l$'s outcome relative to the change in $g_e$'s outcome
  between the same two calendar periods. We can equivalently measure each
  cohort's outcome change relative to the \emph{never-treated} group as a
  common benchmark, and then take the difference. The never-treated benchmark
  cancels because it enters with the same sign in both terms.

  The crucial distinction between the two terms on the right-hand side of
  \eqref{eq:identity} is the treatment status of the \emph{focal} cohort at the
  pre-period $g_l - m$:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $\hbeta^{\infty m}_{g_l,g_l+k}$: focal cohort $g_l$ is
      \emph{untreated} at $g_l - m$ (since $m \geq 1$). This is a valid clean
      pre-treatment vs.\ post-treatment comparison for $g_l$.
    \item $\hpsi^{\infty m}_{g_e,g_l+k}$: focal cohort $g_e$ is
      \emph{already treated} at $g_l - m$ (since $j > m$ implies
      $g_e = g_l - j < g_l - m$). The control group is clean (never-treated),
      but the comparison spans two \emph{post-treatment} periods for $g_e$.
      This term does not identify $g_e$'s treatment effect; it measures the
      \emph{change} in $g_e$'s treatment effect across the two periods.
  \end{itemize}
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Redundancy of Forbidden Comparisons]
  \label{cor:redundancy}
  Every forbidden comparison $\hbeta^{jm}_{g_l,g_l+k}$ lies in the linear
  span of $\{\hbeta^{\infty m}_{g_l,g_l+k},\,\hpsi^{\infty m}_{g_e,g_l+k}\}$.
  Consequently:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item Forbidden comparisons carry no identifying information for the CATTs
      beyond what the never-treated and not-yet-treated comparisons already
      contain.
    \item Dropping the already-treated control comparisons from the moment set
      does not reduce the information available for estimating $\theta$; it
      only changes the weights placed on the available information.
    \item The GB decomposition can be rewritten as an exact signed weighted sum
      of clean DiDs alone (Theorem~\ref{thm:clean_gb}).
  \end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

%%%============================================================================
\section{The Clean Goodman--Bacon Decomposition}
\label{sec:clean_gb}
%%%============================================================================

We apply Lemma~\ref{lem:identity} to every forbidden term in \eqref{eq:gb},
replacing each $\hDelta_{g_l,g_e}^{F}$ with the corresponding difference of
never-treated DiDs. This yields an exact representation of TWFE that involves
only comparisons against never-treated or not-yet-treated control cohorts.

\begin{theorem}[Clean Goodman--Bacon Decomposition]
  \label{thm:clean_gb}
  Under Assumptions~1--2, applying the identity \eqref{eq:identity} to every
  forbidden term in the Goodman-Bacon decomposition \eqref{eq:gb} yields
  \begin{equation}
    \TWFE
    \;=\;
    \underbrace{%
      \sum_{g\neq\infty}w_{g\infty}\,\hDelta_{g,\infty}
      +\sum_{g_e<g_l}w_{g_e g_l}^{E}\,\hDelta_{g_e,g_l}^{E}
    }_{\text{(I) original clean terms}}
    +
    \underbrace{%
      \sum_{j\in\mathcal{F}}v_j\,\hbeta^{\infty m_j}_{g_l^j,\,g_l^j+k_j}
    }_{\text{(II) positive substituted terms}}
    -
    \underbrace{%
      \sum_{j\in\mathcal{F}}v_j\,\hpsi^{\infty m_j}_{g_e^j,\,g_l^j+k_j}
    }_{\text{(III) negative substituted terms}},
    \label{eq:clean_gb_raw}
  \end{equation}
  where $\mathcal{F}$ indexes all forbidden pairs, $v_j = w_{g_l^j g_e^j}^F$
  is the GB weight on forbidden pair $j$, and $(k_j, m_j)$ are the horizon
  and pre-lag of the forbidden comparison. Every DiD on the right-hand side
  uses either the never-treated group or a not-yet-treated cohort as control.
  The identity holds to machine precision in every finite sample.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  For each forbidden pair $j$ with weight $v_j$, Lemma~\ref{lem:identity}
  gives
  \[
    v_j\,\hDelta_{g_l^j,g_e^j}^{F}
    \;=\;
    v_j\,\hbeta^{\infty m_j}_{g_l^j,g_l^j+k_j}
    -v_j\,\hpsi^{\infty m_j}_{g_e^j,g_l^j+k_j}.
  \]
  Summing over all $j\in\mathcal{F}$ and substituting into \eqref{eq:gb}
  yields \eqref{eq:clean_gb_raw}. The exactness of each application of
  Lemma~\ref{lem:identity} propagates through the sum.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Structure of the New Effective Weights}
\label{sec:weights}

The decomposition \eqref{eq:clean_gb_raw} contains three groups of terms. We
characterise their weight structure and identification content.

\paragraph{Group~(I): Original clean terms.}
These are the clean DiDs that appear directly in the GB decomposition---never-
treated and not-yet-treated comparisons. Their weights $w_{g\infty}$ and
$w_{g_e g_l}^E$ are the original GB weights, unchanged by the substitution.

\paragraph{Group~(II): Positive substituted terms.}
For each forbidden pair $j$, the term $\hbeta^{\infty m_j}_{g_l^j, g_l^j+k_j}$
is a standard never-treated DiD for focal cohort $g_l^j$. At the pre-period
$g_l^j - m_j$, cohort $g_l^j$ is untreated (since $m_j \geq 1$), so this is a
valid pre-treatment vs.\ post-treatment comparison that identifies
$\beta_{g_l^j, g_l^j+k_j}$ under Assumptions~1--4. It enters with positive
weight $+v_j$.

\paragraph{Group~(III): Negative substituted terms.}
For each forbidden pair $j$, the term $\hpsi^{\infty m_j}_{g_e^j, g_l^j+k_j}$
is a never-treated DiD for focal cohort $g_e^j$, but evaluated at the
\emph{same calendar periods} as the original forbidden comparison: post period
$g_l^j + k_j$ and pre period $g_l^j - m_j$. Since $j > m$ implies
$g_e^j = g_l^j - j < g_l^j - m_j$, cohort $g_e^j$ is \emph{already treated}
at the pre-period $g_l^j - m_j$. This comparison therefore spans two
post-treatment periods for $g_e^j$, with clean (never-treated) trend control.
It enters with \emph{negative} weight $-v_j$.

The distinction between Groups~(II) and~(III) is entirely about the treatment
status of the \emph{focal} cohort at the pre-period of the comparison:
Group~(II) DiDs are pre-to-post comparisons (focal cohort untreated in pre);
Group~(III) DiDs are post-to-post comparisons (focal cohort treated in both
periods). The identification implications of this distinction are the subject of
Section~\ref{sec:hetero}.

\paragraph{Sum of signed weights.}
The collection of signed weights in \eqref{eq:clean_gb_raw} satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{g\neq\infty}w_{g\infty}
  +\sum_{g_e<g_l}w_{g_e g_l}^{E}
  +\sum_{j\in\mathcal{F}}v_j
  -\sum_{j\in\mathcal{F}}v_j
  \;=\;
  \sum_{g\neq\infty}w_{g\infty}
  +\sum_{g_e<g_l}w_{g_e g_l}^{E}
  \;<\;1,
  \label{eq:wgt_sum}
\end{equation}
strictly less than one whenever any forbidden pair has positive GB weight. TWFE
is therefore not a convex combination of clean DiDs; it is a signed linear
combination of clean DiDs that numerically equals the convex combination of all
DiDs (including forbidden ones) given in Theorem~\ref{thm:gb}.

\subsection{Period-Specific Representation}
\label{sec:period_specific}

Since $\hDelta_{g,\infty}$ is defined in \eqref{eq:did_nt} as the
equal-weighted average of all $(T-g+1)(g-1)$ period-specific never-treated
DiDs $\hbeta^{\infty m}_{g,g+k}$, the GB cohort weight $w_{g\infty}$
distributes uniformly over these building blocks. The period-specific GB weight
on the individual never-treated DiD $\hbeta^{\infty m}_{g,g+k}$ contributed
by Group~(I) is therefore
\begin{equation}
  \omega^{\mathrm{GB}}_{g,k,m}
  \;=\;
  \frac{w_{g\infty}}{(T-g+1)(g-1)},
  \label{eq:ps_weight}
\end{equation}
which is \emph{equal across all $(k,m)$ pairs} within a given cohort $g$:
the GB cohort weight $w_{g\infty}\propto N_gN_\infty(T-g+1)(g-1)/T^2$ is
spread uniformly over the $(T-g+1)(g-1)$ period-by-lag pairs. An analogous
equal-weighted decomposition holds for the not-yet-treated DiDs in Group~(I).

This period-specific view places the clean GB decomposition in the same
language as the heterogeneity-robust estimators of Callaway and Sant'Anna
(2021) and Gardner et al.\ (2024), which we compare in
Section~\ref{sec:comparison}.

%%%============================================================================
\section{Bias Under Heterogeneous Treatment Effects}
\label{sec:hetero}
%%%============================================================================

We now analyse what the clean decomposition~\eqref{eq:clean_gb_raw} reveals
when CATTs are heterogeneous. The central question is whether the clean
Goodman-Bacon decomposition recovers an unbiased estimator. The answer is no:
it is an exact algebraic restatement of TWFE and therefore carries the same
bias. The clean decomposition's contribution is to make the \emph{mechanism}
of that bias explicit through the structure of the negative-weight terms.

\subsection{Expected Values of the Three Groups}

We compute expected values under Assumptions~1--4 without imposing
homogeneity.

\paragraph{Group~(I) terms.}
Clean DiDs identify CATTs regardless of heterogeneity. For the never-treated
comparison,
\[
  \E\bigl[\hbeta^{\infty m}_{g,g+k}\bigr] \;=\; \beta_{g,g+k},
\]
because cohort $g$ is untreated at the pre-period $g-m$ (by Assumptions~2 and~4)
and the never-treated group has $\beta_{\infty,t}=0$ always. The same holds for
the not-yet-treated comparison $\hbeta^{lm}_{g,g+k}$, since the control cohort
$g+l$ is untreated at post-period $g+k < g+l$.

\paragraph{Group~(II) terms.}
Each positive substituted term $\hbeta^{\infty m_j}_{g_l^j, g_l^j+k_j}$ is a
standard never-treated DiD for cohort $g_l^j$. Under Assumptions~1--4,
\begin{equation}
  \E\bigl[\hbeta^{\infty m_j}_{g_l^j,\,g_l^j+k_j}\bigr]
  \;=\;
  \beta_{g_l^j,\,g_l^j+k_j},
  \label{eq:pos_ev}
\end{equation}
which is the CATT of the later-treated cohort at horizon $k_j$. This is an
unbiased estimate of the true parameter of interest.

\paragraph{Group~(III) terms.}
Each negative substituted term $\hpsi^{\infty m_j}_{g_e^j, g_l^j+k_j}$ uses
never-treated as control but compares $g_e^j$'s outcome across two
\emph{post-treatment} calendar periods: $g_l^j + k_j$ (post) and $g_l^j - m_j$
(pre). Since $g_e^j$ is already treated at both periods, under parallel trends
the outcome levels include the cohort's treatment effect at each period. The
expected value is
\begin{equation}
  \E\bigl[\hpsi^{\infty m_j}_{g_e^j,\,g_l^j+k_j}\bigr]
  \;=\;
  \beta_{g_e^j,\,g_l^j+k_j} - \beta_{g_e^j,\,g_l^j-m_j}.
  \label{eq:neg_ev}
\end{equation}
This is the \emph{change} in cohort $g_e^j$'s own CATT between the two
post-treatment periods, not the CATT itself. Clean control group
notwithstanding, this term does not identify any parameter of interest.

\subsection{The Bias Formula}

Taking expectations in \eqref{eq:clean_gb_raw} and substituting
\eqref{eq:pos_ev}--\eqref{eq:neg_ev}:
\begin{align}
  \E[\TWFE]
  &\;=\;
  \underbrace{%
    \sum_{g\neq\infty}w_{g\infty}\,\E[\hDelta_{g,\infty}]
    +\sum_{g_e<g_l}w_{g_e g_l}^{E}\,\E[\hDelta_{g_e,g_l}^{E}]
    +\sum_{j\in\mathcal{F}}v_j\,\beta_{g_l^j,\,g_l^j+k_j}
  }_{\text{unbiased part: identifies CATTs}}
  \notag\\[6pt]
  &\quad
  -\underbrace{%
    \sum_{j\in\mathcal{F}}v_j
    \bigl(\beta_{g_e^j,\,g_l^j+k_j} - \beta_{g_e^j,\,g_l^j-m_j}\bigr)
  }_{\text{bias: weighted change in already-treated cohorts' CATTs}}.
  \label{eq:ev_decomp}
\end{align}
The bias of TWFE under heterogeneous effects is therefore
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{Bias}(\TWFE)
  \;=\;
  -\sum_{j\in\mathcal{F}}
    v_j\,\bigl(\beta_{g_e^j,\,g_l^j+k_j} - \beta_{g_e^j,\,g_l^j-m_j}\bigr),
  \label{eq:bias_formula}
\end{equation}
a weighted sum of changes in the already-treated control cohorts' own CATTs,
where the change is between the post- and pre-periods of the corresponding
forbidden comparison. This is consistent with---and derived from---the single-
comparison bias formula \eqref{eq:bias}.

\subsection{Condition for Unbiasedness}

Equation~\eqref{eq:bias_formula} is zero if and only if
\begin{equation}
  \beta_{g_e^j,\,g_l^j+k_j} = \beta_{g_e^j,\,g_l^j-m_j}
  \qquad\text{for all forbidden pairs }j\in\mathcal{F}.
  \label{eq:unbias_cond}
\end{equation}
This requires that each already-treated control cohort's CATT does not change
between the two calendar periods of the forbidden comparison.

Two cases warrant emphasis:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Homogeneous, time-constant effects} ($\beta_{g,t}=\tau$ for all
    $g,t$): condition~\eqref{eq:unbias_cond} holds trivially, since
    $\tau - \tau = 0$. TWFE is unbiased. This is the ``best case'' for TWFE and
    the assumption under which it was designed.

  \item \textbf{Cohort-heterogeneous but time-constant effects}
    ($\beta_{g,t}=\beta_g$ for all $t$): each CATT depends on cohort but not
    on calendar time, so $\beta_{g_e,g_l+k} - \beta_{g_e,g_l-m} = \beta_{g_e}
    - \beta_{g_e} = 0$. Condition~\eqref{eq:unbias_cond} still holds and TWFE
    is unbiased. Heterogeneity \emph{across cohorts} alone is not sufficient to
    bias TWFE; the critical ingredient is heterogeneity \emph{over time} within
    a cohort.

  \item \textbf{Time-varying effects} ($\beta_{g,t}$ changes with $t$ for any
    cohort $g_e^j$ that serves as already-treated control): the bias is
    generically non-zero. The direction depends on the sign of
    $\beta_{g_e,g_l+k} - \beta_{g_e,g_l-m}$ (whether effects are growing or
    decaying) and can be positive or negative.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Why the Clean Decomposition Does Not Remove Bias}

The clean Goodman-Bacon decomposition \eqref{eq:clean_gb_raw} is an exact
algebraic restatement of TWFE: it evaluates to the same number in every finite
sample. Whatever bias TWFE carries under heterogeneous effects, the clean
decomposition carries identically, because the two are numerically equal.

What has changed is the \emph{representation}, not the estimator. The
negative-weight terms $-v_j\,\hpsi^{\infty m_j}_{g_e^j, g_l^j+k_j}$ were
always implicitly present in TWFE through the forbidden comparisons; the clean
decomposition makes them explicit. Their non-zero expectations under
time-varying effects---given by \eqref{eq:neg_ev}---are precisely TWFE's bias,
now visibly attributed to their source: the change in already-treated cohorts'
own CATTs over the comparison window.

\subsection{What an Unbiased Estimator Requires}

Equation~\eqref{eq:ev_decomp} shows that the ``unbiased part'' of TWFE, when
expressed in clean terms, consists of Groups~(I) and~(II): the original clean
GB terms and the positive substituted never-treated DiDs. These identify CATTs.
The Group~(III) terms introduce bias.

To construct an unbiased estimator, one must:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Zero out the negative-weight terms.} Set $v_j = 0$ for all
    Group~(III) terms, equivalently discarding the contaminating
    $\hpsi^{\infty m_j}_{g_e^j, g_l^j+k_j}$ comparisons. This breaks
    numerical equality with TWFE and produces a genuinely different estimator.
  \item \textbf{Re-normalise the remaining positive weights.} The collection of
    Group~(I) and Group~(II) terms uses only clean DiDs with positive weights,
    but their implicit aggregation reflects the GB variance weighting rather
    than the researcher's target $\theta$. Assigning weights that match the
    aggregation scheme in \eqref{eq:att} gives an unbiased estimator of
    $\theta$.
\end{enumerate}
This two-step procedure is precisely what heterogeneity-robust estimators
implement. Callaway and Sant'Anna (2021) and Sun and Abraham (2021) restrict
to never-treated or not-yet-treated controls and choose aggregation weights
explicitly. Gardner et al.\ (2024) and Borusyak et al.\ (2024) impute
counterfactuals from never-treated observations, implicitly zeroing out the
Group~(III) terms. In all cases, the identifying information comes entirely
from the Group~(I) and Group~(II) comparison types, consistent with
Corollary~\ref{cor:redundancy}: the already-treated control comparisons are
redundant for identification, and their removal does not sacrifice any
information about $\theta$.

%%%============================================================================
\section{Comparison with Callaway--Sant'Anna and Gardner}
\label{sec:comparison}
%%%============================================================================

The aggregate DiD definition \eqref{eq:did_nt} and the
weight~\eqref{eq:ps_weight} express the unbiased part of the clean GB
decomposition---Groups~(I) and~(II) with Group~(III) zeroed out---as a
weighted sum of never-treated $2\times2$ DiDs $\hbeta^{\infty m}_{g,g+k}$
indexed by cohort $g$, event time $k$, and pre-lag $m$. This common language
makes precise how clean GB differs from Callaway and Sant'Anna (2021; CS) and
Gardner et al.\ (2024) along three dimensions: the treatment of the pre-lag
$m$, the aggregation over event time $k$, and the cohort weighting scheme.

\subsection{Callaway and Sant'Anna (2021)}
\label{sec:cs}

CS estimate each CATT $\beta_{g,g+k}$ using a \emph{single} pre-period lag
$m=1$:
\begin{equation}
  \hbeta^{CS}_{g,g+k}
  \;\equiv\;
  \hbeta^{\infty 1}_{g,\,g+k}
  \;=\;
  (Y_{g,g+k}-Y_{g,g-1})-(Y_{\infty,g+k}-Y_{\infty,g-1}).
  \label{eq:cs_catt}
\end{equation}
The researcher then applies chosen cohort-and-event weights $w^{CS}_{g,g+k}$
to aggregate across $(g,k)$ cells:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\theta}^{CS}
  \;=\;
  \sum_{g\neq\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{T-g}
  w^{CS}_{g,g+k}\,\hbeta^{\infty 1}_{g,\,g+k}.
  \label{eq:cs_agg}
\end{equation}
CS also supports not-yet-treated units as an alternative control group in
place of, or in addition to, the never-treated group.

\paragraph{Difference from clean GB.}
Both estimators use only clean controls and are unbiased under heterogeneous
effects. The key difference is in the $m$-dimension. CS places all weight on
$m=1$ and zero weight on $m\geq2$. The period-specific clean GB weight
$\omega^{GB}_{g,k,m}=w_{g\infty}/[(T-g+1)(g-1)]$ is spread equally across
all $m\in\{1,\ldots,g-1\}$, making full use of all pre-treatment periods.
In the $k$-dimension, CS allows the researcher to choose $w^{CS}_{g,g+k}$
freely, while clean GB inherits the GB cohort weight $w_{g\infty}$ distributed
equally over $k$.

\paragraph{Advantages of CS relative to clean GB.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Researcher-specified aggregation.} The weights $w^{CS}_{g,g+k}$
    can target any aggregate ATT---event-study curves, calendar-time averages,
    or cohort-specific effects---without being constrained to the GB variance-
    based weighting scheme.
  \item \textbf{Period-specific CATTs.} CS delivers $\hbeta^{CS}_{g,g+k}$ as
    an explicit output for each $(g,k)$ cell, enabling event-study plots and
    formal tests of treatment effect dynamics. Clean GB produces only the
    cohort-level aggregate $\hDelta_{g,\infty}$.
  \item \textbf{Uniform pre-trend diagnostic.} Using the same estimand
    $\hbeta^{\infty 1}_{g,g+k}$ at negative horizons $k<0$ provides a natural
    and consistently defined pre-trend test across the full event window.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Disadvantages of CS relative to clean GB.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Efficiency loss from discarding pre-periods.} By using only
    $m=1$, CS ignores all pre-treatment periods $g-2,g-3,\ldots,1$. Under
    parallel trends each such period provides valid identifying variation;
    averaging over all pre-lags (as clean GB and Gardner do) reduces variance
    without biasing the estimate. For a cohort treated at $g$ with $g\geq3$,
    the gain from averaging grows with $g-1$.
  \item \textbf{Sensitivity to the base period.} The estimand
    $\hbeta^{\infty 1}_{g,g+k}$ is anchored to the single pre-treatment period
    $g-1$. If that period is atypical due to measurement noise, anticipation
    effects, or seasonal patterns, the estimates are sensitive to this choice.
    Averaging over multiple pre-lags dilutes such sensitivity.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Gardner et al.\ (2024)}
\label{sec:gardner}

Gardner et al.\ (2024) estimate CATTs by a two-stage procedure: unit and time
fixed effects are estimated on untreated observations (never-treated units and
pre-treatment periods of treated units), and treatment-effect residuals are
then regressed on cohort-by-event-time indicators. Under parallel trends with
never-treated as control, the Gardner CATT estimator equals the equal-weighted
average of never-treated DiDs over all pre-lags:
\begin{equation}
  \hbeta^{G}_{g,g+k}
  \;\equiv\;
  \frac{1}{g-1}\sum_{m=1}^{g-1}\hbeta^{\infty m}_{g,\,g+k}.
  \label{eq:gardner_catt}
\end{equation}
Applying researcher-chosen weights $w^G_{g,g+k}$, the aggregate Gardner
estimator is
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\theta}^{G}
  \;=\;
  \sum_{g\neq\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{T-g}
  w^G_{g,g+k}\,\hbeta^{G}_{g,g+k}
  \;=\;
  \sum_{g\neq\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{T-g}\sum_{m=1}^{g-1}
  \frac{w^G_{g,g+k}}{g-1}\,\hbeta^{\infty m}_{g,\,g+k}.
  \label{eq:gardner_agg}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Difference from clean GB.}
Gardner and clean GB agree on the $m$-dimension: both average over all pre-lags
$m\in\{1,\ldots,g-1\}$ with equal weight within each cohort. They differ in
how event time $k$ and cohort weights are handled. Clean period-specific GB
assigns equal weight across all $k$ within a cohort ($\omega^{GB}_{g,k,m}$
is constant in $k$), with cohort weight $w_{g\infty}\propto
N_gN_\infty(T-g+1)(g-1)/T^2$. Gardner allows $w^G_{g,g+k}$ to vary
freely over $(g,k)$. Setting $w^G_{g,g+k}=w_{g\infty}/(T-g+1)$---uniform
over $k$ within each cohort and equal to the GB cohort weights---makes the
two estimators numerically identical.

\paragraph{Advantages of Gardner relative to clean GB.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Researcher-specified event-study weights.} Like CS, Gardner
    decouples estimation ($\hbeta^G_{g,g+k}$) from aggregation (the weights
    $w^G_{g,g+k}$). The researcher can target specific event horizons or
    compute treatment dynamics that clean GB does not directly deliver.
  \item \textbf{Period-specific CATTs.} $\hbeta^G_{g,g+k}$ is defined at the
    $(g,k)$ cell level, enabling event-study analysis of how effects evolve
    since treatment onset.
  \item \textbf{Full use of pre-treatment information.} By averaging over all
    $m$, Gardner is (weakly) more efficient than CS in the pre-lag dimension
    for any cohort with $g\geq3$.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Disadvantages of Gardner relative to clean GB.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Non-GB cohort weights.} The cohort weight implied by constant
    $w^G_{g,g+k}$ is proportional to $N_g(T-g+1)$, omitting the
    $N_\infty(g-1)$ factor in $w_{g\infty}$. Neither scheme is uniformly
    superior; the appropriate choice depends on the target estimand.
  \item \textbf{Two-stage inference.} First-stage FE estimation introduces
    additional sampling uncertainty requiring inference adjustment---typically
    a bootstrap---at greater computational cost than the single-stage clean GB
    decomposition.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Summary}
\label{sec:comparison_summary}

Table~\ref{tab:comparison} collects the key differences.

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering
\caption{Clean GB, CS, and Gardner along the period-specific representation}
\label{tab:comparison}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Feature & Clean GB (Groups~I+II) & CS & Gardner \\
\midrule
Control group & Never- and not-yet-treated & Never- or not-yet-treated & Never-treated \\

\bottomrule

\noindent
The three estimators span a trade-off between transparency and flexibility.
Clean GB is directly linked to the algebra of TWFE---revealing exactly which
comparisons drive its behaviour and where its bias originates---but it produces
only the cohort-level aggregate $\hDelta_{g,\infty}$ rather than
period-specific CATTs. CS and Gardner decouple estimation from aggregation,
giving the researcher control over event-time and cohort weights, at the cost
of requiring explicit aggregation decisions.

Within the class of estimators that average over all pre-lags $m$, Gardner
and clean GB produce the same CATT estimates and differ only in the $(g,k)$
weighting. Within the class of estimators that fix $m=1$, CS is the canonical
representative. The efficiency cost of restricting to $m=1$ is borne whenever
a cohort has more than one pre-treatment period.

None of the three estimators exploits the covariance structure across clean
moments induced by shared cohorts and within-unit serial correlation.
Section~\ref{sec:gmm} develops a GMM framework that resolves this by combining
all clean DiD moments with an optimal weighting matrix, achieving minimum
asymptotic variance within the class of clean-DiD estimators.

%%%============================================================================
\section{A GMM Estimator Based on Clean Comparisons}
\label{sec:gmm}
%%%============================================================================

Corollary~\ref{cor:redundancy} establishes that all identifying information
for the treatment parameter is encoded in the clean $2\times2$ DiDs. Every
heterogeneity-robust estimator in Section~\ref{sec:comparison} exploits this
by restricting to clean comparisons, but each uses a fixed weighting scheme
that leaves efficiency gains unrealised. This section develops a GMM framework
that selects weights to minimise asymptotic variance.

Two cases are distinguished. Under \emph{homogeneous effects}
($\beta_{g,g+k}=\theta$ for all $g,k$), the parameter is a scalar and every
clean comparison identifies the same $\theta$ (Section~\ref{sec:gmm_homog}).
Under \emph{heterogeneous effects}, CATTs vary across cohort-event cells and
the parameter of interest is a researcher-specified weighted average of the
CATT vector. In both cases the GMM uses only clean comparisons: forbidden
comparisons are algebraically redundant and excluded throughout
(Section~\ref{sec:gmm_hetero}, Remark~\ref{rem:redund_hetero}).

%%%------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Homogeneous Effects}
\label{sec:gmm_homog}
%%%------------------------------------------------------------------------

Under homogeneous effects ($\beta_{g,g+k}=\theta$ for all $g,k$), every
clean comparison is an unbiased estimator of the same scalar $\theta$.
The GMM uses all $N_c$ clean DiDs as moments and selects the weighting
matrix to minimise asymptotic variance. The efficiency analysis
distinguishes two error regimes: spherical errors
(Section~\ref{sec:gmm_spherical}) and non-spherical errors with serial
correlation (Section~\ref{sec:gmm_nonspherical}).

\subsubsection{Moment Conditions}
\label{sec:gmm_moments}

The defining identity of a $2\times2$ DiD under the true parameter provides
the moment conditions. For units $i\in g$ and $j\in c$ with $c$ clean
(never-treated or not-yet-treated), the individual-level moment is
\begin{equation}
  \phi^{cm}_{g,g+k}
  \;=\;
  \bigl[(Y_{ig,g+k}-Y_{ig,g-m})-(Y_{jc,g+k}-Y_{jc,g-m})\bigr]
  -\theta,
  \label{eq:moment_indiv}
\end{equation}
with $\E[\phi^{cm}_{g,g+k}]=0$ under Assumptions~1--4 and homogeneous
effects. The sample analog at the cohort level is
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\phi}^{cm}_{g,g+k}(\theta)
  \;=\;
  \hbeta^{cm}_{g,g+k} - \theta,
  \qquad
  \E\bigl[\hat{\phi}^{cm}_{g,g+k}(\theta)\bigr] = 0,
  \label{eq:moment}
\end{equation}
for every clean pair $(g,c)$---i.e., $c=\infty$ (never-treated) or
$c=g+l$, $l\in\{k+1,\ldots,T-g\}$ (not-yet-treated)---and all valid
$(k,m)$. Let $N_c$ denote the total number of clean $2\times2$
comparisons. Stacking all $N_c$ sample moments into a column vector,
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\phi}(\theta)
  \;=\;
  \Delta_c - \mathbf{1}_{N_c}\,\theta,
  \label{eq:stacked}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta_c$ is the $N_c\times1$ vector of sample clean DiD estimates
and $\mathbf{1}_{N_c}$ is the $N_c\times1$ vector of ones. The condition
$\E[\hat{\phi}(\theta_0)]=\mathbf{0}$ holds at the true $\theta_0$.

The vector $\mathbf{1}_{N_c}$ plays the role of the incidence matrix: it
maps every clean comparison to the single scalar parameter $\theta$. Since
$N_c>1$ generically, the system is over-identified, providing the
information that GMM exploits to improve upon any single-comparison
or fixed-weight estimator.

\subsubsection{The GMM Estimator}
\label{sec:gmm_estimator}

The GMM estimator minimises the quadratic form of sample moments,
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{GMM}}
  \;=\;
  \arg\min_{\theta}\;
  \hat{\phi}(\theta)'\,A\,\hat{\phi}(\theta),
  \label{eq:gmm_obj}
\end{equation}
where $A$ is an $N_c\times N_c$ positive semi-definite weighting matrix.
Setting the first-order condition to zero gives the closed form
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{%
    \hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{GMM}}
    \;=\;
    \bigl(\mathbf{1}_{N_c}'\,A\,\mathbf{1}_{N_c}\bigr)^{-1}
    \mathbf{1}_{N_c}'\,A\,\Delta_c.
  }
  \label{eq:gmm}
\end{equation}
This is a \emph{weighted average of clean DiD estimates}: the effective weight
on comparison $s$ is $(A\,\mathbf{1}_{N_c})_s / (\mathbf{1}_{N_c}'A\mathbf{1}_{N_c})$.
Different choices of $A$ recover the estimators of
Section~\ref{sec:comparison} as special cases; their relationship to the
full heterogeneous GMM is discussed in Section~\ref{sec:gmm_connection}.

\subsubsection{Efficiency under Spherical Errors}
\label{sec:gmm_spherical}

Under \emph{spherical errors}, $\mathrm{Var}[\varepsilon_{igt}]=\sigma^2$
and $\mathrm{Cov}[\varepsilon_{igt},\varepsilon_{igt'}]=0$ for all $t\neq t'$.
At the cohort level this implies $\mathrm{Var}[Y_{g,t}]=\sigma^2/N_g$ and
$\mathrm{Cov}[Y_{g,t},Y_{g,t'}]=0$ for $t\neq t'$, with cohorts independent
across $g$.

\paragraph{Covariance structure.}
Expanding the clean DiD $\hbeta^{cm}_{g,g+k}=(Y_{g,g+k}-Y_{g,g-m})-(Y_{c,g+k}-Y_{c,g-m})$
and using cross-sectional and serial independence, the covariance between two
clean comparisons sharing the same focal cohort ($g=g'$) and control ($c=c'$)
is
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{Cov}\!\bigl[\hbeta^{cm}_{g,g+k},\hbeta^{cm'}_{g,g+k'}\bigr]
  \;=\;
  \sigma^2\!\left(\frac{1}{N_g}+\frac{1}{N_c}\right)
  \!\bigl(\mathbf{1}_{[k=k']}+\mathbf{1}_{[m=m']}\bigr),
  \label{eq:cov_sph}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{1}_{[\cdot]}$ denotes the indicator. When both indices coincide
($k=k'$, $m=m'$) this gives the variance $2\sigma^2(1/N_g+1/N_c)$.  For
different focal cohorts ($g\neq g'$) sharing only the same never-treated
control ($c=c'=\infty$), the covariance arises solely through shared
never-treated outcomes:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{Cov}\!\bigl[\hbeta^{\infty m}_{g,g+k},\hbeta^{\infty m'}_{g',g'+k'}\bigr]
  \;=\;
  \frac{\sigma^2}{N_\infty}
  \bigl(\mathbf{1}_{[g+k=g'+k']}+\mathbf{1}_{[g-m=g'-m']}\bigr).
  \label{eq:cov_sph_cross}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Exchangeable within-cell structure.}
Fix cohort $g$ and event horizon $k$. The $g-1$ clean never-treated DiDs
$\{\hbeta^{\infty m}_{g,g+k}\}_{m=1}^{g-1}$ all use the same post-period
outcome $Y_{g,g+k}$; under spherical errors this creates a uniform pairwise
covariance. Setting $v_{g,c}=\sigma^2(1/N_g+1/N_c)$, their
$(g-1)\times(g-1)$ covariance matrix is
\begin{equation}
  \Sigma_{g,k}
  \;=\;
  v_{g,\infty}\bigl(I_{g-1}+\mathbf{1}_{g-1}\mathbf{1}_{g-1}'\bigr),
  \label{eq:sigma_block}
\end{equation}
with every off-diagonal entry equal to $v_{g,\infty}$ (shared post-period)
and every diagonal entry equal to $2v_{g,\infty}$. This is an
\emph{exchangeable} (equicorrelated) structure.

\begin{proposition}[Spherical-Error Optimality of Gardner's CATT Estimate]
  \label{prop:gardner_opt}
  Under spherical errors, the minimum-variance linear unbiased estimator
  of $\beta_{g,g+k}$ from the clean never-treated DiDs
  $\{\hbeta^{\infty m}_{g,g+k}\}_{m=1}^{g-1}$ is the equal-weighted average
  \begin{equation}
    \hbeta^{G}_{g,g+k}
    \;=\;
    \frac{1}{g-1}\sum_{m=1}^{g-1}\hbeta^{\infty m}_{g,g+k},
    \label{eq:gardner_opt}
  \end{equation}
  the Gardner et al.\ (2024) CATT estimator.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  The GLS estimator of the common mean from observations with covariance
  $\Sigma_{g,k}$ in \eqref{eq:sigma_block} is
  $\hat\theta^{*}=(\mathbf{1}'\Sigma_{g,k}^{-1}\mathbf{1})^{-1}
  \mathbf{1}'\Sigma_{g,k}^{-1}\Delta_{g,k}$, where
  $\Delta_{g,k}=(\hbeta^{\infty1}_{g,g+k},\ldots,\hbeta^{\infty,g-1}_{g,g+k})'$.
  Applying the Sherman--Morrison formula to \eqref{eq:sigma_block},
  \[
    \Sigma_{g,k}^{-1}
    \;=\;
    \frac{1}{v_{g,\infty}}
    \!\left(I_{g-1}-\frac{\mathbf{1}_{g-1}\mathbf{1}_{g-1}'}{g}\right)\!,
  \]
  so $\mathbf{1}'\Sigma_{g,k}^{-1}=v_{g,\infty}^{-1}\mathbf{1}'/g$ and
  $\mathbf{1}'\Sigma_{g,k}^{-1}\mathbf{1}=(g-1)/(v_{g,\infty}g)$.
  Substituting:
  \[
    \hat\theta^{*}
    =
    \frac{v_{g,\infty}^{-1}\mathbf{1}'\Delta_{g,k}/g}{(g-1)/(v_{g,\infty}g)}
    =
    \frac{\mathbf{1}'\Delta_{g,k}}{g-1}
    =
    \frac{1}{g-1}\sum_{m=1}^{g-1}\hbeta^{\infty m}_{g,g+k}
    =\hbeta^{G}_{g,g+k}.\qedhere
  \]
\end{proof}

The result has a clean intuition: under an exchangeable covariance
structure the GLS estimator of a common mean equals the OLS (equal-weighted)
average, because the uniform pairwise covariance cancels in the GLS formula.
Spherical errors therefore provide no reason to downweight any particular
pre-lag, and Gardner's equal-weighted average is the efficient choice.

\begin{remark}
  Proposition~\ref{prop:gardner_opt} characterises within-cell estimation.
  The full optimal GMM for the scalar $\theta$ additionally exploits the
  cross-cell covariances in \eqref{eq:cov_sph_cross}. The asymptotic
  variance of $\hbeta^G_{g,g+k}$ under spherical errors is
  $gv_{g,\infty}/(g-1)$, so the optimal cross-cell weight is proportional
  to $(g-1)/[g\sigma^2(1/N_g+1/N_\infty)]$---a specific function of
  cohort sizes and number of pre-treatment periods that neither CS nor
  Gardner imposes.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{Efficiency under Non-Spherical Errors}
\label{sec:gmm_nonspherical}

Under within-unit serial correlation, $\mathrm{Cov}[\varepsilon_{igt},
\varepsilon_{igt'}]\neq0$ for $t\neq t'$, and the covariance structure of
$\Delta_c$ is richer than \eqref{eq:cov_sph}--\eqref{eq:cov_sph_cross}.
The equal-weighted within-cell average is no longer optimal, and the full
inverse $\Omega_\phi^{-1}$ is required for efficient estimation. We derive
$\Omega_\phi$ under three increasingly structured assumptions on the error
process.

\paragraph{Arbitrary Serial Correlation.}
\label{sec:gmm_arbitrary}

Without parametric restrictions on the serial dependence, the asymptotic
moment covariance matrix is
\begin{equation}
  \Omega_\phi
  \;=\;
  \lim_{N\to\infty}
  \mathrm{Var}\!\bigl[\sqrt{N}\,\hat{\phi}(\theta_0)\bigr]
  \;=\;
  \lim_{N\to\infty} N\cdot\mathrm{Var}[\Delta_c].
  \label{eq:omega}
\end{equation}
Setting $A=\hat{\Omega}_\phi^{-1}$ for a consistent estimator
$\hat{\Omega}_\phi$ gives the feasible optimal estimator
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{GMM}}^{*}
  \;=\;
  \bigl(\mathbf{1}_{N_c}'\,\hat{\Omega}_\phi^{-1}\,\mathbf{1}_{N_c}\bigr)^{-1}
  \mathbf{1}_{N_c}'\,\hat{\Omega}_\phi^{-1}\,\Delta_c,
  \label{eq:opt_gmm}
\end{equation}
which achieves the minimum asymptotic variance
$(\mathbf{1}_{N_c}'\Omega_\phi^{-1}\mathbf{1}_{N_c})^{-1}$
among all linear unbiased estimators of $\theta$. Under
Assumption~1, $\hat{\Omega}_\phi$ can be estimated non-parametrically
by the cluster-robust sandwich variance estimator applied to the stacked
moments $\hat{\phi}(\hat{\theta})$, clustering at the unit level.

\paragraph{Toeplitz Structure with Homoskedasticity.}
\label{sec:gmm_toeplitz_homo}

A practically useful parametric restriction imposes time-stationarity and
homoskedasticity: $\mathrm{Var}[Y_{igt}]=\sigma_0$ and
$\mathrm{Cov}[Y_{igt},Y_{igt-r}]=\sigma_r$ for all $g,t$ and all $|r|\geq1$,
with $\sigma_r=\sigma_0$ when $r=0$. This Toeplitz structure reduces the
number of free parameters from $\binom{n+1}{2}$ (generic covariance)
to $n$ (one autocovariance per lag).

\paragraph{Diagonal elements.}
For clean comparison $s=(g,c,k,m)$, let $d=k+m$ denote the calendar
distance between its post-period $g+k$ and pre-period $g-m$. The variance
is
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{Var}\!\bigl[\hat\Delta^{s}_{g,g+k}\bigr]
  \;=\;
  \left(\frac{2}{N_g}+\frac{2}{N_c}\right)(\sigma_0-\sigma_d).
  \label{eq:var_toeplitz}
\end{equation}
Under spherical errors ($\sigma_r=0$ for $r\geq1$, $\sigma_0=\sigma^2$),
\eqref{eq:var_toeplitz} reduces to $2\sigma^2(1/N_g+1/N_c)$, consistent
with Section~\ref{sec:gmm_spherical}.

\paragraph{Off-diagonal elements.}
For two clean comparisons $s=(g,c,k,m)$ and $s'=(g',c',k',m')$, define
\begin{equation}
  \rho(k,m,k',m')
  \;\equiv\;
  \sigma_{|k-k'|}-\sigma_{|k-m'|}-\sigma_{|m-k'|}+\sigma_{|m-m'|},
  \label{eq:rho}
\end{equation}
where $|k-m'|=k+m'$ and $|m-k'|=m+k'$ are the calendar distances between
the post-period of one comparison and the pre-period of the other
(since $k\geq0$, $m\geq1$). The asymptotic off-diagonal entries of
$\Omega_\phi$ (as $N\to\infty$ with $N_g/N\to p_g$ fixed) are:
\begin{equation}
  \lim_{N\to\infty}N\cdot\mathrm{Cov}\!\bigl[\hat\Delta^s,\hat\Delta^{s'}\bigr]
  \;=\;
  \begin{cases}
    0
    & g\neq g',\;c\neq c',\;\{g,c\}\cap\{g',c'\}=\emptyset,\\[6pt]
    \phantom{+}\rho(k,m,k',m')
    & g=g',\;c\neq c'\;\text{ or }\;g\neq g',\;c=c',\\[6pt]
    -\rho(k,m,k',m')
    & g'=c,\;g\neq c'\;\text{ (or symmetric)},\\[6pt]
    2\,\rho(k,m,k',m')
    & g=g',\;c=c'.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:cov_toeplitz}
\end{equation}
Three features are notable. First, comparisons involving entirely disjoint
cohort sets are asymptotically uncorrelated: $\Omega_\phi$ is block-sparse.
Second, sharing only the focal cohort (or only the control) contributes
$\rho$; the factor doubles to $2\rho$ when both cohorts are shared, because
both the focal and control outcome changes appear in both comparisons.
Third, the cross-comparison ($g'=c$) case---where one comparison's focal
cohort is the other's control---contributes $-\rho$, because the shared
cohort enters with opposite signs in the two DiDs.

Under spherical errors ($\sigma_r=0$ for $r\geq1$), $\rho(k,m,k',m')=
\sigma_0(\mathbf{1}_{[k=k']}+\mathbf{1}_{[m=m']})$ for the same-cohort case,
recovering \eqref{eq:cov_sph}--\eqref{eq:cov_sph_cross}.

\paragraph{Estimation.}
The parameters $\sigma_0$ and $\{\sigma_r\}_{r\geq1}$ are unknown. A
consistent estimator can be obtained by running a first-stage TWFE regression,
extracting unit-level residuals $\hat{\varepsilon}_{igt}$, and computing
$\hat\sigma_r=(NT)^{-1}\sum_{i,t}\hat\varepsilon_{igt}\hat\varepsilon_{igt-r}$.
Substituting into \eqref{eq:var_toeplitz}--\eqref{eq:cov_toeplitz} yields
$\hat\Omega_\phi$.

\paragraph{Toeplitz Structure with Heteroskedasticity.}
\label{sec:gmm_toeplitz_hetero}

Allowing the variance to vary across cohort-time cells while keeping the
autocovariance structure stationary:
$\mathrm{Var}[Y_{igt}]=\sigma_{g,t}^{2}$ and
$\mathrm{Cov}[Y_{igt},Y_{igt-r}]=\sigma_r$ for all $r\geq1$. This extends
the number of variance parameters from one ($\sigma_0$) to $GT$ (one per
cohort-time cell) while leaving the $n$ autocovariance parameters
$\{\sigma_r\}_{r\geq1}$ unchanged, raising the total parameter count from
$n$ to $2n-1$.

\paragraph{Diagonal elements.}
For comparison $s=(g,c,k,m)$ with calendar distance $d=k+m$:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{Var}\!\bigl[\hat\Delta^{s}_{g,g+k}\bigr]
  \;=\;
  \frac{\sigma^{2}_{g,g+k}+\sigma^{2}_{g,g-m}-2\sigma_d}{N_g}
  +
  \frac{\sigma^{2}_{c,g+k}+\sigma^{2}_{c,g-m}-2\sigma_d}{N_c}.
  \label{eq:var_hetero}
\end{equation}
Under homoskedasticity ($\sigma^2_{g,t}=\sigma_0$ for all $g,t$),
\eqref{eq:var_hetero} reduces to \eqref{eq:var_toeplitz}. The off-diagonal
structure is unchanged: the asymptotic covariances take the same
form~\eqref{eq:cov_toeplitz} with $\rho(k,m,k',m')$ as defined in
\eqref{eq:rho}, because autocovariances depend only on the lag.

\paragraph{Practical trade-off.}
Heteroskedasticity raises the estimation cost: $\sigma_{g,t}^2$ must be
estimated for each cohort-time pair, introducing $GT-1$ additional nuisance
parameters. In finite samples the additional estimation uncertainty may
offset the efficiency gains from correctly specifying the variance structure.
In practice, the homoskedastic Toeplitz specification of
Section~\ref{sec:gmm_toeplitz_homo} offers a favourable bias-variance
trade-off for moderate $T$ and $G$.

\subsection{Heterogeneous Effects}
\label{sec:gmm_hetero}
%%%------------------------------------------------------------------------

Under heterogeneous effects, CATTs $\beta_{g,g+k}$ vary across cohort-event
cells and the parameter of interest is a researcher-specified weighted average
of the CATT vector. By Corollary~\ref{cor:redundancy} and its heterogeneous
extension (see below), forbidden comparisons carry no identifying information
beyond the clean ones and are excluded from the moment set throughout.

\subsubsection{The CATT Vector and Aggregate ATT}
\label{sec:gmm_hetero_catt}

Let $\boldsymbol{\beta}=(\beta_{g,g+k})_{g,k}$ denote the $N_\beta\times1$
vector of CATTs, indexed over all treated cohorts $g$ and event horizons
$k\geq0$. The aggregate ATT is a researcher-specified linear combination
\begin{equation}
  \theta
  \;=\;
  \mathbf{w}'\boldsymbol{\beta}
  \;=\;
  \sum_{g}\sum_{k\geq0} w_{g,g+k}\,\beta_{g,g+k},
  \qquad
  \sum_{g,k}w_{g,g+k}=1,
  \label{eq:att_hetero}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{w}$ is the $N_\beta\times1$ vector of aggregation weights
chosen by the researcher. Standard choices include equal weighting across all
$(g,k)$ cells (overall ATT), cohort-specific weighting (cohort ATTs), or
weighting by cell size to match the TWFE-implicit scheme.

\subsubsection{Moment Conditions}
\label{sec:gmm_hetero_moments}

For each clean comparison $(g,c,k,m)$ with $c\in\{\infty,\,g+l\}$
(never-treated or not-yet-treated), the bias formula \eqref{eq:bias}
gives $\E[\hbeta^{cm}_{g,g+k}]=\beta_{g,g+k}$. The moment condition is
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\phi}^{cm}_{g,g+k}(\boldsymbol{\beta})
  \;=\;
  \hbeta^{cm}_{g,g+k} - \beta_{g,g+k},
  \label{eq:moment_clean_h}
\end{equation}
with $\E[\hat{\phi}^{cm}_{g,g+k}(\boldsymbol{\beta}_0)]=0$ at the true
parameter. Stacking all $N_c$ clean moments into a column vector:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\boldsymbol{\beta})
  \;=\;
  \boldsymbol{\Delta}_c - Q_c\,\boldsymbol{\beta},
  \label{eq:stacked_h}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\Delta}_c$ is the $N_c\times1$ vector of clean DiD
estimates and $Q_c$ is the $N_c\times N_\beta$ \emph{clean incidence matrix}.
Each row of $Q_c$ has $+1$ in the column for $\beta_{g,g+k}$ (the CATT
targeted by that comparison) and $0$ elsewhere. In the homogeneous case
$N_\beta=1$ and $Q_c=\mathbf{1}_{N_c}$, recovering \eqref{eq:stacked}.
The system \eqref{eq:stacked_h} is identified whenever $Q_c$ has full
column rank, which holds if every $\beta_{g,g+k}$ is anchored by at least
one clean comparison.

\begin{remark}[Redundancy of Forbidden Comparisons under Heterogeneous Effects]
  \label{rem:redund_hetero}
  Forbidden comparisons are excluded because they add no new moment
  conditions beyond what the clean set already provides. Applying
  Lemma~\ref{lem:identity} to the sample identity
  $\hbeta^{jm}_{g_l,g_l+k}=\hbeta^{\infty m}_{g_l,g_l+k}-
  \hpsi^{\infty m}_{g_e,g_l+k}$, and noting that
  $\hpsi^{\infty m}_{g_e,g_l+k}=\hbeta^{\infty m'}_{g_e,g_l+k}-
  \hbeta^{\infty m'}_{g_e,g_l-m}$ for any pre-lag $m'$, the
  \emph{debiased} forbidden moment equals an exact linear combination of
  three clean never-treated moments:
  \[
    \hat{\phi}^{jm}_{g_l,g_l+k}(\boldsymbol{\beta})
    \;=\;
    \hat{\phi}^{\infty m}_{g_l,\,g_l+k}(\boldsymbol{\beta})
    -\hat{\phi}^{\infty m'}_{g_e,\,g_l+k}(\boldsymbol{\beta})
    +\hat{\phi}^{\infty m'}_{g_e,\,g_l-m}(\boldsymbol{\beta}).
  \]
  Adding linearly dependent moments to a GMM system leaves the estimator
  and its asymptotic variance unchanged. Forbidden comparisons are therefore
  redundant in both the homogeneous (Corollary~\ref{cor:redundancy}) and
  heterogeneous cases.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{The GMM Estimator}
\label{sec:gmm_hetero_estimator}

The GMM estimator minimises the quadratic form over clean moments,
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
  \;=\;
  \arg\min_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\;
  \hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\boldsymbol{\beta})'\,A\,
  \hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}(\boldsymbol{\beta}),
  \label{eq:gmm_h_obj}
\end{equation}
where $A$ is an $N_c\times N_c$ positive semi-definite weighting matrix.
The first-order conditions yield the closed form
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{%
    \hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
    \;=\;
    \bigl(Q_c'\,A\,Q_c\bigr)^{-1}Q_c'\,A\,\boldsymbol{\Delta}_c,
  }
  \label{eq:gmm_h}
\end{equation}
and the aggregate ATT estimator is $\hat{\theta}=\mathbf{w}'\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}$.
The asymptotic distribution satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \sqrt{N}\,(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}-\boldsymbol{\beta})
  \;\xrightarrow{d}\;
  \mathcal{N}\!\Bigl(
    \mathbf{0},\;
    (Q_c'AQ_c)^{-1}Q_c'A\,\Omega_c\,AQ_c(Q_c'AQ_c)^{-1}
  \Bigr),
  \label{eq:asy_h}
\end{equation}
where $\Omega_c=\lim_{N\to\infty}N\cdot\mathrm{Var}[\boldsymbol{\Delta}_c]$
is the asymptotic covariance of the clean DiD vector. At the optimal
$A=\Omega_c^{-1}$ the sandwich simplifies to $(Q_c'\Omega_c^{-1}Q_c)^{-1}$.

\subsubsection{Efficiency and Optimality}
\label{sec:gmm_hetero_efficiency}

\begin{proposition}[Efficiency of Optimal GMM under Heterogeneous Effects]
  \label{prop:gmm_hetero_opt}
  Let $\mathcal{L}_c=\{L\boldsymbol{\Delta}_c: L\in\mathbb{R}^{N_\beta\times
  N_c},\; LQ_c=I_{N_\beta}\}$ denote the class of all linear unbiased
  estimators of $\boldsymbol{\beta}$ based on clean DiDs. The GMM estimator
  \eqref{eq:gmm_h} with $A=\Omega_c^{-1}$ achieves the minimum asymptotic
  variance within $\mathcal{L}_c$:
  \[
    \mathrm{AsyVar}[L\boldsymbol{\Delta}_c]
    \;\geq\;
    (Q_c'\,\Omega_c^{-1}\,Q_c)^{-1}
    \qquad\text{for all }L\boldsymbol{\Delta}_c\in\mathcal{L}_c,
  \]
  with equality if and only if
  $L=(Q_c'\Omega_c^{-1}Q_c)^{-1}Q_c'\Omega_c^{-1}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Any $L\boldsymbol{\Delta}_c\in\mathcal{L}_c$ satisfies $LQ_c=I_{N_\beta}$
  and has asymptotic variance $L\,\Omega_c\,L'$. Write $L=L^*+(L-L^*)$
  where $L^*=(Q_c'\Omega_c^{-1}Q_c)^{-1}Q_c'\Omega_c^{-1}$. The constraint
  $LQ_c=I_{N_\beta}$ forces $(L-L^*)Q_c=\mathbf{0}$, so the cross-term
  $(L-L^*)\Omega_c L^{*\prime}=(L-L^*)Q_c(Q_c'\Omega_c^{-1}Q_c)^{-1}
  =\mathbf{0}$. Then
  \[
    L\,\Omega_c\,L'
    \;=\;
    L^*\Omega_c L^{*\prime}
    + (L-L^*)\Omega_c(L-L^*)'
    \;\geq\;
    L^*\Omega_c L^{*\prime}
    \;=\;
    (Q_c'\Omega_c^{-1}Q_c)^{-1}.\qedhere
  \]
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Connection to Earlier Estimators}
\label{sec:gmm_connection}

The estimators of Section~\ref{sec:comparison} are members of
$\mathcal{L}_c$ that impose restrictions on the weighting matrix $A$.

\paragraph{Callaway--Sant'Anna.}
CS restricts to never-treated controls and uses only the $m=1$ pre-lag.
Each CATT is estimated from a single comparison $\hbeta^{\infty1}_{g,g+k}$,
discarding the efficiency gains from additional pre-period moments ($m\geq2$)
without any benefit from forbidden comparisons, which are redundant by
Remark~\ref{rem:redund_hetero}.

\paragraph{Gardner et al.\ (2024).}
Gardner's CATT estimator $\hbeta^G_{g,g+k}=(g-1)^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^{g-1}
\hbeta^{\infty m}_{g,g+k}$ uses all pre-lags with never-treated controls.
Within each $(g,k)$ cell it assigns equal weight across pre-lags, which is
optimal under spherical errors (Proposition~\ref{prop:gardner_opt}) but not
under non-spherical errors. The gap between Gardner and the optimal GMM
arises entirely from sub-optimal within-cell weighting and the failure to
exploit cross-moment serial covariances.

\paragraph{Optimal GMM.}
Setting $A=\hat{\Omega}_c^{-1}$ achieves the efficiency bound of
Proposition~\ref{prop:gmm_hetero_opt}. Gains relative to Gardner are
concentrated in cohorts with many pre-treatment periods and in panels with
high serial persistence, where different pre-lags carry substantially
different amounts of orthogonal information about the same CATT.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
%%%============================================================================

\subsection{How TWFE Accommodates Forbidden Comparisons}

In the original GB representation \eqref{eq:gb}, TWFE assigns positive weight
to every $2\times2$ DiD, including forbidden ones. The mechanism is the
within-pair variance weighting that the OLS projection naturally generates:
any two cohorts that differ in their treatment timing produce variation in the
treatment indicator, and OLS exploits all such variation proportionally to its
magnitude. Using already-treated cohorts as controls extends the set of
comparisons and reduces variance---under homogeneous, time-constant effects this
is costless, because the bias term $\beta_{g_e,g_l+k}-\beta_{g_e,g_l-m}$ in
\eqref{eq:bias} vanishes. TWFE's reliance on forbidden comparisons is an
implicit optimisation for the homogeneous-effects case.

\subsection{A Taxonomy of Estimators by Effective Weight}

The clean decomposition \eqref{eq:clean_gb_raw} and the bias
formula~\eqref{eq:bias_formula} suggest a natural taxonomy:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{TWFE}: assigns positive weight to Groups~(I) and~(II) and
    negative weight to Group~(III). Biased under time-varying heterogeneous
    effects; efficient under homogeneous effects (Gauss--Markov).

  \item \textbf{Heterogeneity-robust estimators} (CS, SA, Gardner, Borusyak):
    assign zero weight to Group~(III) and positive weight to Groups~(I)
    and~(II) only. Unbiased under heterogeneous effects; potentially
    inefficient because the covariance structure of clean moments is ignored.
    Section~\ref{sec:comparison} compares CS and Gardner in detail along the
    pre-lag and event-time dimensions of the period-specific representation.

  \item \textbf{Optimal GMM} (Section~\ref{sec:gmm}): uses clean moments
    with $A=\hat{\Omega}_\phi^{-1}$ to minimise asymptotic variance in both
    the homogeneous (Section~\ref{sec:gmm_homog}) and heterogeneous
    (Section~\ref{sec:gmm_hetero}) cases. Forbidden comparisons are
    redundant by Corollary~\ref{cor:redundancy} and its heterogeneous
    extension (Remark after Proposition~\ref{prop:gmm_hetero_opt});
    efficiency gains over Gardner arise solely from optimal weighting across
    pre-lags and exploitation of cross-moment serial covariances.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Summary}

The Goodman-Bacon decomposition expresses TWFE as a weighted average of all
$2\times2$ DiDs, with positive weight on forbidden comparisons.
Lemma~\ref{lem:identity} establishes that every forbidden comparison---using an
already-treated control---equals the difference of two never-treated DiDs at the
same calendar periods, with the never-treated terms cancelling exactly.
Corollary~\ref{cor:redundancy} shows that forbidden comparisons are therefore
redundant: they carry no identifying information beyond the clean comparisons.
Theorem~\ref{thm:clean_gb} rewrites TWFE as a signed sum of clean DiDs,
recovering TWFE to machine precision. Section~\ref{sec:hetero} shows that the
negative-weight terms---never-treated DiDs for already-treated focal cohorts
across two post-treatment periods---are zero in expectation under homogeneous
constant effects and non-zero under time-varying heterogeneous effects. The bias
of TWFE equals minus the weighted sum of changes in already-treated cohorts'
CATTs (equation~\eqref{eq:bias_formula}). An unbiased estimator must zero out
the Group~(III) terms and re-normalise the remaining positive-weight clean DiDs
to target the desired aggregate ATT. Section~\ref{sec:comparison} shows how
this construction relates to Callaway and Sant'Anna (2021) and Gardner
et al.\ (2024) in the period-specific representation, highlighting where the
estimators agree---use of clean controls, and averaging over all pre-lags in
the case of Gardner---and where they differ in their treatment of event-time
weights and cohort weighting schemes.
