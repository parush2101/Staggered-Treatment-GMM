\title{%
  Redundancy of Forbidden Comparisons\\
  and a Clean Goodman--Bacon Decomposition%
}
\author{Parush Arora \and Rishabh Bijani\\
  \small Department of Economics, Ashoka University}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
The Goodman--Bacon (2021) decomposition shows that the two-way fixed effects
(TWFE) estimator is a weighted average of \emph{all} pairwise $2\times 2$
difference-in-differences (DiD) comparisons, including \emph{forbidden} ones
that use already-treated cohorts as controls. We prove a sample-level
algebraic identity: every forbidden DiD equals the difference of two
never-treated DiDs computed over the same time window, with the never-treated
terms cancelling exactly. This identity implies that forbidden comparisons are
\emph{redundant}---they contain no information beyond what is already encoded
in the clean comparisons. Exploiting the identity, we derive a \emph{clean
Goodman--Bacon decomposition} that represents TWFE as a signed weighted sum of
clean DiDs alone, recovering TWFE exactly (to machine precision) in every
sample. We characterise the new effective weights and show that already-treated
cohorts enter with \emph{negative} effective weight, exposing the structural
mechanism through which TWFE becomes biased and less efficient under
heterogeneous treatment effects.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup and Notation}
\label{sec:setup}
%%%============================================================================

We observe the balanced panel $\{Y_{igt},D_{it}\}_{i=1}^{N}$ over $T$
calendar periods indexed $t\in\{1,\ldots,T\}$. Here $Y_{igt}\in\mathbb{R}$ is
the outcome of unit $i$ in cohort $g$ at time $t$, and $D_{it}\in\{0,1\}$ is a
binary treatment indicator. Units are partitioned into cohorts indexed by their
first treatment date. We let $g=\infty$ index the \emph{never-treated} cohort
and $g\in\{2,\ldots,T\}$ index the $G$ treated cohorts. Let $N_g$ denote the
number of units in cohort $g$, with $\sum_{g}N_g=N$. The within-cohort average
outcome at time $t$ is
\begin{equation}
  Y_{g,t} \;=\; \frac{1}{N_g}\sum_{i\in g}Y_{igt}.
  \label{eq:cohort_avg}
\end{equation}
For any set of calendar periods $\mathcal{S}$, let $\bY_{g,\mathcal{S}}=
|\mathcal{S}|^{-1}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{S}}Y_{g,t}$ denote the cohort--period
average.

\medskip
We maintain four standard assumptions.

\begin{assumption}[Cross-Sectional Independence]
  $\{Y_{ig1},\ldots,Y_{igT},D_{i1},\ldots,D_{iT}\}_{i=1}^{N}$ are
  independent and identically distributed across $i$; serial dependence
  within units is unrestricted.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Irreversibility of Treatment]
  $D_{i,t-1}=1$ implies $D_{it}=1$ for all $i,t$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Parallel Trends]
  For all cohorts $g,g'$ and all $t>t'$:
  \[
    \E[Y_{igt}(\infty)]-\E[Y_{igt'}(\infty)]
    \;=\;
    \E[Y_{ig't}(\infty)]-\E[Y_{ig't'}(\infty)],
  \]
  where $Y_{igt}(\infty)$ denotes the potential outcome under no treatment.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[No Anticipation]
  $\E[Y_{igt}\mid D_{it}=0]=\E[Y_{igt}(\infty)]$ for all $t<g$.
\end{assumption}

\subsection*{Cohort-and-time ATT and aggregate ATT}

The cohort-and-time-specific average treatment effect on the treated (CATT)
for cohort $g$ at event horizon $k\geq0$ is
\begin{equation}
  \beta_{g,g+k}
  \;=\;
  \E\!\bigl[Y_{ig,g+k}(1)-Y_{ig,g+k}(\infty)\mid G_i=g\bigr],
  \label{eq:catt}
\end{equation}
where $Y_{igt}(1)$ is the potential outcome under treatment and $G_i=g$ denotes
cohort membership. The aggregate ATT is a researcher-chosen weighted average of
CATTs,
\begin{equation}
  \theta \;=\; \sum_{g}\sum_{k\geq0}w_{g,g+k}\,\beta_{g,g+k},
  \qquad \sum_{g}\sum_{k\geq0}w_{g,g+k}=1,
  \label{eq:att}
\end{equation}
with the standard choice weighting each $(g,t)$ cell by its share of treated
observations. The TWFE regression
\begin{equation}
  Y_{igt} = \alpha_i + \lambda_t + \theta\,D_{it} + \varepsilon_{it}
  \label{eq:twfe}
\end{equation}
produces a consistent estimator of $\theta$ only when CATTs are homogeneous
across all $(g,t)$ cells. Under heterogeneous CATTs, $\TWFE$ is generally biased.

%%%============================================================================
\section{Two-by-Two DiD Comparisons}
\label{sec:2x2}
%%%============================================================================

We index $2\times2$ DiD comparisons by the focal cohort $g$, control cohort
$c$, post-period calendar time $g+k$ ($k\geq0$), and pre-period lag $m\geq1$.
The sample $2\times2$ DiD is
\begin{equation}
  \hbeta^{cm}_{g,g+k}
  \;=\;
  (Y_{g,g+k}-Y_{g,g-m})-(Y_{c,g+k}-Y_{c,g-m}).
  \label{eq:2x2}
\end{equation}
Three types of control cohort arise in the staggered design, leading to the
following classification.

\begin{definition}[Clean and Forbidden Comparisons]
  \label{def:types}
  The $2\times2$ DiD $\hbeta^{cm}_{g,g+k}$ in \eqref{eq:2x2} is:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Never-treated} (clean, $c=\infty$): the control group is
      never treated,
      \[
        \hbeta^{\infty m}_{g,g+k}
        = (Y_{g,g+k}-Y_{g,g-m})-(Y_{\infty,g+k}-Y_{\infty,g-m}).
      \]
    \item \textbf{Not-yet-treated} (clean, $c=g+l$, $l\in\{k+1,\ldots,T-g\}$):
      the control cohort has not yet received treatment by the post period
      $g+k$,
      \[
        \hbeta^{lm}_{g,g+k}
        = (Y_{g,g+k}-Y_{g,g-m})-(Y_{g+l,g+k}-Y_{g+l,g-m}).
      \]
    \item \textbf{Already-treated} (\emph{forbidden}, $c=g-j$,
      $j\in\{m+1,\ldots,g-1\}$): the control cohort has been treated for at
      least $j-m>0$ periods before the pre-period reference date $g-m$,
      \[
        \hbeta^{jm}_{g,g+k}
        = (Y_{g,g+k}-Y_{g,g-m})-(Y_{g-j,g+k}-Y_{g-j,g-m}).
      \]
  \end{enumerate}
  The restriction $j>m$ ensures the already-treated control's first treatment
  date $g-j$ strictly precedes the pre-period reference date $g-m$, so the
  control group is treated throughout both the pre- and post-windows of the
  comparison.
\end{definition}

Under Assumptions~1--4, all three types satisfy
$\E[\hbeta^{cm}_{g,g+k}]=\beta_{g,g+k}$ when CATTs are homogeneous. Under
heterogeneous CATTs, however, the expected value of the forbidden comparison
is
\begin{equation}
  \E\bigl[\hbeta^{jm}_{g,g+k}\bigr]
  \;=\;
  \beta_{g,g+k}
  - \bigl(\beta_{g-j,\,g+k} - \beta_{g-j,\,g-m}\bigr),
  \label{eq:bias}
\end{equation}
so the bias equals the change in the control cohort's own CATT between the
pre- and post-periods. Clean comparisons do not suffer this contamination
because $\beta_{c,t}=0$ for all $t$ when $c=\infty$, and $\beta_{c,t}=0$
for $t<c$ when $c=g+l$ (the not-yet-treated control has not yet been exposed
to treatment).

%%%============================================================================
\section{The Goodman--Bacon Decomposition}
\label{sec:gb}
%%%============================================================================

Goodman-Bacon (2021) establishes that $\TWFE$ is a weighted average of all
pairwise $2\times2$ DiDs that can be formed from the panel data. To state the
decomposition precisely, we define aggregate DiD objects that average outcomes
over entire time windows.

\subsection*{Aggregate DiD objects}

For each treated cohort $g$, let $\Tpost_g=\{g,g+1,\ldots,T\}$ and
$\Tpre_g=\{1,2,\ldots,g-1\}$ denote its full post- and pre-treatment calendar
periods. The \textbf{treated-vs-never-treated DiD} is
\begin{equation}
  \hDelta_{g,\infty}
  \;=\;
  (\bY_{g,\Tpost_g}-\bY_{g,\Tpre_g})
  -(\bY_{\infty,\Tpost_g}-\bY_{\infty,\Tpre_g}).
  \label{eq:did_nt}
\end{equation}

For an early--late pair $(g_e,g_l)$ with $g_e<g_l$, define the
\emph{mid-period} $\Mint_{el}=\{g_e,\ldots,g_l-1\}$ (cohort $g_e$ is treated,
$g_l$ is not yet treated) and the \emph{post-period}
$\Post_{el}=\{g_l,\ldots,T\}$ (both cohorts are treated). Two $2\times2$ DiDs
arise from this pair:
\begin{align}
  \hDelta_{g_e,g_l}^{E}
  &\;=\;
  (\bY_{g_e,\Mint_{el}}-\bY_{g_e,\Tpre_{g_e}})
  -(\bY_{g_l,\Mint_{el}}-\bY_{g_l,\Tpre_{g_e}}),
  \label{eq:did_early}
  \\[4pt]
  \hDelta_{g_l,g_e}^{F}
  &\;=\;
  (\bY_{g_l,\Post_{el}}-\bY_{g_l,\Mint_{el}})
  -(\bY_{g_e,\Post_{el}}-\bY_{g_e,\Mint_{el}}).
  \label{eq:did_forbidden}
\end{align}
The superscript $E$ (``Earlier vs.\ Later Treated'') denotes the clean
comparison: $g_l$ is not yet treated during $\Mint_{el}$, so it is a valid
control. The superscript $F$ (``Later vs.\ Earlier Treated'') denotes the
forbidden comparison: $g_e$ is already treated throughout both $\Mint_{el}$
and $\Post_{el}$, so it is a contaminated control.

\subsection*{The decomposition}

\begin{theorem}[Goodman-Bacon Decomposition; Goodman-Bacon, 2021]
  \label{thm:gb}
  Under Assumptions~1--2, the TWFE estimator satisfies
  \begin{equation}
    \TWFE
    \;=\;
    \sum_{g\neq\infty}w_{g\infty}\,\hDelta_{g,\infty}
    \;+\;
    \sum_{g_e<g_l}\!
      \Bigl[
        w_{g_e g_l}^{E}\,\hDelta_{g_e,g_l}^{E}
        +
        w_{g_l g_e}^{F}\,\hDelta_{g_l,g_e}^{F}
      \Bigr],
    \label{eq:gb}
  \end{equation}
  where all weights are non-negative and sum to one:
  \[
    \sum_{g\neq\infty}w_{g\infty}
    +\sum_{g_e<g_l}\!\bigl(w_{g_e g_l}^{E}+w_{g_l g_e}^{F}\bigr)
    \;=\;1.
  \]
  The weights depend on cohort sizes and treatment timing. For the
  treated-vs-never-treated term,
  \begin{equation}
    w_{g\infty}
    \;\propto\;
    N_g N_\infty \cdot
    \frac{|\Tpost_g|\cdot|\Tpre_g|}{T^2},
    \label{eq:wgt_nt}
  \end{equation}
  where $|\mathcal{S}|$ denotes the number of periods in set $\mathcal{S}$.
  The cohort-pair weights $w_{g_e g_l}^{E}$ and $w_{g_l g_e}^{F}$ are
  similarly proportional to the product of sub-panel sizes and the within-panel
  variance of the treatment indicator; see Goodman-Bacon (2021, Theorem~1) for
  explicit formulas.
\end{theorem}

The decomposition makes explicit the implicit weighting that TWFE performs.
Crucially, the forbidden DiDs $\hDelta_{g_l,g_e}^{F}$ receive strictly positive
weight $w_{g_l g_e}^{F}>0$ whenever the panel contains both cohorts. Thus
TWFE genuinely relies on already-treated controls, and under heterogeneous
CATTs this induces bias via \eqref{eq:bias}.

%%%============================================================================
\section{Redundancy of Forbidden Comparisons}
\label{sec:redundancy}
%%%============================================================================

We now establish the central result of this note. Every forbidden DiD is an
\emph{exact algebraic function} of two clean never-treated DiDs computed over
the same time window. The identity is a sample-level fact---it holds in every
realisation of the data, not merely in expectation.

\begin{lemma}[Algebraic Identity for Forbidden DiDs]
  \label{lem:identity}
  Let $(g_e,g_l)$ be any early--late pair with $g_e<g_l$ and let
  $\Mint_{el}=\{g_e,\ldots,g_l-1\}$ and $\Post_{el}=\{g_l,\ldots,T\}$ be the
  mid- and post-periods defined in Section~\ref{sec:gb}. Define the
  \emph{window-specific never-treated DiD} for cohort $g\in\{g_e,g_l\}$:
  \begin{equation}
    \hdelta_{g,\infty}^{el}
    \;\equiv\;
    (\bY_{g,\Post_{el}}-\bY_{g,\Mint_{el}})
    -(\bY_{\infty,\Post_{el}}-\bY_{\infty,\Mint_{el}}).
    \label{eq:window_did}
  \end{equation}
  Then the following algebraic identity holds in every finite sample:
  \begin{equation}
    \boxed{%
      \hDelta_{g_l,g_e}^{F}
      \;=\;
      \hdelta_{g_l,\infty}^{el}
      \;-\;
      \hdelta_{g_e,\infty}^{el}.
    }
    \label{eq:identity}
  \end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Expand the right-hand side directly:
  \begin{align*}
    \hdelta_{g_l,\infty}^{el}-\hdelta_{g_e,\infty}^{el}
    &\;=\;
    \Bigl[(\bY_{g_l,\Post_{el}}-\bY_{g_l,\Mint_{el}})
          -(\bY_{\infty,\Post_{el}}-\bY_{\infty,\Mint_{el}})\Bigr]
    \\
    &\quad
    -\Bigl[(\bY_{g_e,\Post_{el}}-\bY_{g_e,\Mint_{el}})
           -(\bY_{\infty,\Post_{el}}-\bY_{\infty,\Mint_{el}})\Bigr]
    \\
    &\;=\;
    (\bY_{g_l,\Post_{el}}-\bY_{g_l,\Mint_{el}})
    -(\bY_{g_e,\Post_{el}}-\bY_{g_e,\Mint_{el}})
    \\
    &\quad
    +\underbrace{%
      (\bY_{\infty,\Post_{el}}-\bY_{\infty,\Mint_{el}})
      -(\bY_{\infty,\Post_{el}}-\bY_{\infty,\Mint_{el}})
    }_{=\;0}
    \\
    &\;=\;
    (\bY_{g_l,\Post_{el}}-\bY_{g_l,\Mint_{el}})
    -(\bY_{g_e,\Post_{el}}-\bY_{g_e,\Mint_{el}})
    \;=\;\hDelta_{g_l,g_e}^{F},
  \end{align*}
  where the last equality follows from definition \eqref{eq:did_forbidden}.
  The never-treated terms $\bY_{\infty,\Post_{el}}-\bY_{\infty,\Mint_{el}}$
  cancel \emph{exactly} in every sample because they appear with opposite signs.
  No approximation, probability limit, or expectation is invoked.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  The identity \eqref{eq:identity} has a transparent interpretation. A
  forbidden DiD compares the outcome change of $g_l$ against that of $g_e$
  over the window $[\Mint_{el},\Post_{el}]$. Each cohort's outcome change
  relative to the never-treated group constitutes a valid clean DiD
  ($\hdelta_{g_l,\infty}^{el}$ and $\hdelta_{g_e,\infty}^{el}$, respectively).
  Taking their difference eliminates the common never-treated trend, leaving
  precisely the cross-cohort comparison that defines the forbidden DiD. The
  cancellation is algebraic---not statistical---because the never-treated
  group enters symmetrically in both terms.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Redundancy of Forbidden Comparisons]
  \label{cor:redundancy}
  Every forbidden DiD $\hDelta_{g_l,g_e}^{F}$ lies in the linear span of
  the clean never-treated DiDs
  $\{\hdelta_{g,\infty}^{el}:g\in\{g_l,g_e\}\}$. Consequently:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item Forbidden comparisons carry no identifying information for the CATTs
      beyond what the clean comparisons already contain.
    \item Dropping the forbidden comparisons from the moment set does not
      reduce the information available for estimating $\theta$; it only
      changes the weights placed on the available information.
    \item The GB decomposition can be rewritten as an exact weighted sum of
      clean DiDs alone (Theorem~\ref{thm:clean_gb} below).
  \end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

%%%============================================================================
\section{The Clean Goodman--Bacon Decomposition}
\label{sec:clean_gb}
%%%============================================================================

We apply Lemma~\ref{lem:identity} to every forbidden term in the GB
decomposition \eqref{eq:gb}, replacing each $\hDelta_{g_l,g_e}^{F}$ with
$\hdelta_{g_l,\infty}^{el}-\hdelta_{g_e,\infty}^{el}$. This substitution is
exact and produces a representation of TWFE that involves \emph{only} clean
DiDs.

\begin{theorem}[Clean Goodman--Bacon Decomposition]
  \label{thm:clean_gb}
  Under Assumptions~1--2, the TWFE estimator satisfies
  \begin{equation}
    \TWFE
    \;=\;
    \underbrace{%
      \sum_{g\neq\infty}w_{g\infty}\,\hDelta_{g,\infty}
      +\sum_{g_e<g_l}w_{g_e g_l}^{E}\,\hDelta_{g_e,g_l}^{E}
    }_{\text{(I) original clean terms}}
    +
    \underbrace{%
      \sum_{g_e<g_l}w_{g_l g_e}^{F}\,\hdelta_{g_l,\infty}^{el}
    }_{\text{(II) positive substituted terms}}
    -
    \underbrace{%
      \sum_{g_e<g_l}w_{g_l g_e}^{F}\,\hdelta_{g_e,\infty}^{el}
    }_{\text{(III) negative substituted terms}},
    \label{eq:clean_gb_raw}
  \end{equation}
  where every DiD on the right-hand side is clean (uses the never-treated group
  or a not-yet-treated cohort as control). The identity holds to machine
  precision in every finite sample.

  \medskip\noindent
  Collecting terms by cohort--window pair $(g,el)$, define the
  \emph{effective weight} on $\hdelta_{g,\infty}^{el}$ as
  \begin{equation}
    \tilde{w}_{g,el}
    \;=\;
    \sum_{\substack{j\in\mathcal{F}\\g_l^j=g}}\!\!v_j
    \;-\;
    \sum_{\substack{j\in\mathcal{F}\\g_e^j=g}}\!\!v_j,
    \label{eq:eff_wgt}
  \end{equation}
  where $v_j\equiv w_{g_l^j g_e^j}^{F}$ is the GB weight on forbidden pair
  $j$, $\mathcal{F}$ indexes all forbidden pairs, and the first (second) sum
  runs over pairs in which cohort $g$ plays the role of the later-treated
  (earlier-treated) cohort. The clean decomposition is then
  \begin{equation}
    \TWFE
    \;=\;
    \sum_{g\neq\infty}w_{g\infty}\,\hDelta_{g,\infty}
    +\sum_{g_e<g_l}w_{g_e g_l}^{E}\,\hDelta_{g_e,g_l}^{E}
    +\sum_{(g,el)}\tilde{w}_{g,el}\,\hdelta_{g,\infty}^{el},
    \label{eq:clean_gb}
  \end{equation}
  where the last sum is over all cohort--window pairs $(g,el)$ introduced by
  the substitution of forbidden DiDs.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Direct substitution of Lemma~\ref{lem:identity} into \eqref{eq:gb}: for
  each forbidden pair $(g_e,g_l)$ with weight $v_j=w_{g_l g_e}^{F}$,
  \[
    v_j\,\hDelta_{g_l,g_e}^{F}
    \;=\;
    v_j\,\hdelta_{g_l,\infty}^{el}-v_j\,\hdelta_{g_e,\infty}^{el}.
  \]
  Summing over all forbidden pairs and collecting terms yields
  \eqref{eq:clean_gb_raw}. Grouping by cohort--window pair gives the effective
  weights \eqref{eq:eff_wgt} and the compact form \eqref{eq:clean_gb}. The
  exactness of \eqref{eq:identity} propagates to the theorem: every step is
  an algebraic rearrangement with no approximation.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Structure of the New Effective Weights}
\label{sec:weights}

The effective weights in \eqref{eq:eff_wgt} have a distinctive signed
structure that we characterise below.

\paragraph{Positive weights (later-treated cohorts).}
For each forbidden pair $(g_e,g_l)$ with weight $v_j$, cohort $g_l$ receives
a positive contribution $+v_j$ on the window-specific clean DiD
$\hdelta_{g_l,\infty}^{el}$ over $[\Mint_{el},\Post_{el}]$. This term is
interpretable: $g_l$'s outcome change relative to the never-treated group
over the post-window of its own treatment constitutes a valid clean comparison
for estimating $g_l$'s treatment effect.

\paragraph{Negative weights (earlier-treated cohorts).}
Cohort $g_e$ receives a negative contribution $-v_j$ on
$\hdelta_{g_e,\infty}^{el}$. This is the key structural feature: $g_e$ has
been treated throughout both $\Mint_{el}$ and $\Post_{el}$, so its DiD with
the never-treated group captures the \emph{change} in $g_e$'s own treatment
effect across these windows---not a counterfactual trend. The forbidden
comparison implicitly subtracts $g_e$'s own effect, and the clean
decomposition makes this subtraction explicit via the negative weight.

More formally, under the parallel-trends assumption,
\begin{align}
  \E\bigl[\hdelta_{g_l,\infty}^{el}\bigr]
  &\;=\;
  \frac{1}{|\Post_{el}|}\!\sum_{t\in\Post_{el}}\beta_{g_l,t}
  -\frac{1}{|\Mint_{el}|}\!\sum_{t\in\Mint_{el}}\beta_{g_l,t},
  \label{eq:edid_gl}
  \\[4pt]
  \E\bigl[\hdelta_{g_e,\infty}^{el}\bigr]
  &\;=\;
  \frac{1}{|\Post_{el}|}\!\sum_{t\in\Post_{el}}\beta_{g_e,t}
  -\frac{1}{|\Mint_{el}|}\!\sum_{t\in\Mint_{el}}\beta_{g_e,t},
  \label{eq:edid_ge}
\end{align}
so that $\E[\hdelta_{g_l,\infty}^{el}-\hdelta_{g_e,\infty}^{el}]$ equals
the expected forbidden DiD (cf.\ \eqref{eq:bias}). Under homogeneous,
time-constant effects ($\beta_{g,t}=\tau$ for all $g,t$), both
\eqref{eq:edid_gl} and \eqref{eq:edid_ge} equal $\tau-\tau=0$ over any
window in which the cohort is treated throughout both sub-periods, so the
negative terms contribute zero in expectation and TWFE is unbiased. Under
heterogeneous or time-varying CATTs, $\E[\hdelta_{g_e,\infty}^{el}]\neq0$,
giving rise to bias.

\paragraph{Signed-weight representation and non-negative alternatives.}
The clean decomposition \eqref{eq:clean_gb} is a \emph{signed} weighted sum of
clean DiDs: the weights $\tilde{w}_{g,el}$ can be negative. This contrasts
with heterogeneity-robust estimators (e.g., Callaway and Sant'Anna, 2021; Sun
and Abraham, 2021) that restrict all weights to be non-negative. The sum of the
effective weights satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{g\neq\infty}w_{g\infty}
  +\sum_{g_e<g_l}w_{g_e g_l}^{E}
  +\sum_{(g,el)}\tilde{w}_{g,el}
  \;=\;
  \sum_{g\neq\infty}w_{g\infty}
  +\sum_{g_e<g_l}w_{g_e g_l}^{E},
  \label{eq:wgt_sum}
\end{equation}
because the positive and negative effective weights from each forbidden pair
cancel in the aggregate ($+v_j-v_j=0$). The right-hand side of \eqref{eq:wgt_sum}
is strictly less than one whenever forbidden comparisons have positive GB weight,
so the collection of effective weights does not sum to one. TWFE is therefore
not a convex combination of clean DiDs; it is a signed linear combination of
clean DiDs that evaluates to the same number as the convex combination of all
DiDs (including forbidden ones) given by Theorem~\ref{thm:gb}.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
%%%============================================================================

\subsection{How TWFE Accommodates Forbidden Comparisons}

In the original Goodman-Bacon representation \eqref{eq:gb}, TWFE assigns
positive weight to every $2\times2$ DiD, including forbidden ones. The
mechanism is the within-pair variance weighting that the TWFE projection
naturally generates: any two cohorts that differ in their treatment timing
produce a source of identifying variation, and the OLS projection exploits all
such variation proportionally to its magnitude. Using already-treated cohorts
as controls extends the set of comparisons and therefore reduces variance---under
\emph{homogeneous} effects this is costless, because the contaminating term
$\beta_{g_e,g+k}-\beta_{g_e,g-m}$ in \eqref{eq:bias} vanishes when effects
are constant over time. TWFE's reliance on forbidden comparisons is thus an
implicit optimisation for the homogeneous-effects case: it extracts the maximum
variance from the panel under the maintained assumption that all DiDs identify
the same estimand.

The Goodman-Bacon decomposition exposes this implicit choice by showing the
weight that TWFE places on each forbidden comparison. In empirical applications
with substantial heterogeneity, a large share of the total GB weight may fall
on forbidden DiDs (as in Beck et al., 2010, where 87.5\% of the weight in the
full sample comes from forbidden or always-treated comparisons), making the
bias severe.

\subsection{What Changes in the Clean Decomposition}

The clean Goodman-Bacon decomposition does not discard any information, modify
the estimator, or change its numerical value. It is an exact restatement of
TWFE. Three things change in the representation:

\begin{enumerate}

  \item \textbf{Negative effective weights reveal the bias mechanism.}
    The terms $-v_j\,\hdelta_{g_e,\infty}^{el}$ in \eqref{eq:clean_gb_raw}
    enter with negative weight because the forbidden comparison implicitly
    subtracts the already-treated cohort's own outcome change. Under
    heterogeneous CATTs, $\E[\hdelta_{g_e,\infty}^{el}]=
    \bar{\beta}_{g_e,\Post}-\bar{\beta}_{g_e,\Mint}\neq0$, so these terms
    contribute bias to TWFE. The clean decomposition makes this contamination
    explicit: TWFE is biased because the negative-weight terms are
    non-centred under heterogeneity.

  \item \textbf{TWFE is not a weighted average of clean DiDs with positive
    weights.} A researcher building an estimator from clean moments only and
    restricting all weights to be non-negative---as do Callaway and Sant'Anna
    (2021) and Sun and Abraham (2021)---is solving a different optimisation
    problem from TWFE. The clean decomposition \eqref{eq:clean_gb} confirms
    that TWFE, even when expressed entirely in clean terms, involves negative
    weights on $\hdelta_{g_e,\infty}^{el}$ for all $g_e$ that appear as
    already-treated controls.

  \item \textbf{The negative weights add variance without adding
    identification.}
    Under Corollary~\ref{cor:redundancy}, the information content of the
    forbidden comparisons is already present in the clean DiDs. The
    negative-weight terms $\hdelta_{g_e,\infty}^{el}$ therefore contribute
    noise without adding identifying variation. Under serial correlation, this
    additional variance is compounded because $\hdelta_{g_e,\infty}^{el}$
    is correlated with other DiDs that involve the same units. An optimal
    estimator that exploits the covariance structure of the clean moments---as
    in the GMM framework developed in this paper---can therefore achieve lower
    variance than TWFE by assigning zero weight to the contaminating negative
    terms and optimally weighting the positive clean moments.

\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Connection to the Literature and Implications for Estimation}

Callaway and Sant'Anna (2021) and Sun and Abraham (2021) avoid forbidden
comparisons by construction, using never-treated or not-yet-treated cohorts as
controls. Corollary~\ref{cor:redundancy} provides the formal justification for
this approach: the clean comparisons span the same space of identifying
information as all comparisons including forbidden ones, so nothing is lost by
restricting to clean controls.

Gardner et al.\ (2024) and Borusyak et al.\ (2024) impute counterfactuals
from pre-treatment and never-treated observations, which also amounts to using
only clean comparisons. Wooldridge (2025) proposes the flexible TWFE
specification \eqref{eq:twfe_flex} that achieves the same goal through
cohort-by-time interaction terms.

The clean decomposition \eqref{eq:clean_gb} suggests a natural classification
of estimators by their effective weight on $\hdelta_{g_e,\infty}^{el}$:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{TWFE}: effective weight $\tilde{w}_{g_e,el}=-v_j<0$
    (contaminated).
  \item \textbf{Heterogeneity-robust estimators}: effective weight on
    $\hdelta_{g_e,\infty}^{el}$ is zero (clean, unbiased, but potentially
    inefficient under serial correlation because the covariance structure of
    the clean moments is ignored).
  \item \textbf{Optimal GMM}: assigns weights to the clean moments that
    minimise asymptotic variance by exploiting their covariance structure,
    achieving efficiency within the class of unbiased estimators based on
    clean $2\times2$ DiDs.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Summary}

The Goodman-Bacon decomposition expresses TWFE as a weighted average of all
$2\times2$ DiDs, with positive weight on forbidden comparisons.
Lemma~\ref{lem:identity} establishes that every forbidden DiD equals the
difference of two window-specific never-treated DiDs, with the never-treated
terms cancelling exactly in every sample. Corollary~\ref{cor:redundancy}
shows that forbidden comparisons are therefore \emph{redundant}: they lie in
the linear span of the clean DiDs and carry no additional identifying
information. Theorem~\ref{thm:clean_gb} exploits this identity to rewrite
TWFE as a signed weighted sum of clean DiDs, recovering TWFE to machine
precision. The effective weights reveal that already-treated cohorts enter with
negative weight in the clean representation---the structural origin of both the
bias and the efficiency loss of TWFE under heterogeneous treatment effects.
